## Lock One

### "Piers Morgan", senior editor and literary critic

### Score: 7.8 / 10 — NOT YET PASSED (pass mark 8.5)

This is the most emotionally intelligent premise of the winter run. Mrs Quill is a genuine creation: shy, creaky-voiced, avoided because of how she looks, and quietly doing the kindest thing in the Hollow. The story's best device is structural. The custom says "No asking. No thanking." (l.5). It ends with the rabbit kits coming to say thank you, and with Mrs Quill's "Thank you for coming to ask" (l.428). Maggie Mae's notebook, "It's two lines long" (l.209–213), is an excellent way to *show* what was actually known, and it fits her list-making canon perfectly. Reba's "I like finding out what's inside things. Even mittens." (l.381) turns her canon flaw into a gift in one line.

Six problems hold the score down:
- **The first concept flag is not met.** Mrs Quill takes far beyond what the custom allows ("may come and take one", l.5). She strips the tree nightly, including good mittens and the tiny pairs the kits needed that very day (l.56–60). She tells nobody, and the story endorses her secrecy as "easier" (l.311). This is the brief's central safeguard, and the draft models exactly the behaviour the flag excludes. See L1-1.
- **The story is built on the same chassis as S01-007, four stories ago.** Reba does wrong. The story grows as it passes along a chain of neighbours (007 Ch6 "The Fib Grows"; here l.191). Reba confesses to the wronged party. A public announcement declares she is sorry (007 l.344: "IT WAS REBA! SHE'S VERY SORRY!"; here l.346: "And Reba was wrong about her, and is very sorry, and says so"). Everybody helps. Reba's clever paws find their true use (007's shagbark ridges; here, unpicking). The miller's wife is present in both. A listener hearing the season in order will recognise the shape.
- **The moral is spelled out three times before Buck speaks** (house rule 1): by Maggie Mae in Ch5, by Reba in Ch8, and in Buck's own lead-in in Ch10.
- **The mitten counts and the timeline do not add up** (see Lock Two): "Forty-one pairs" (l.184); "all four" when one had already gone (l.170); "all week" for a tree three days old (l.305).
- **The detective plot is dropped, not solved.** Reba's scent clue ("old leaves… a bit of pine", l.93) and Maggie Mae's map (l.95) lead nowhere. The answer arrives by sitting still and watching (Ch4), then by Mrs Quill explaining (Ch7). The children do make the key *moral* move (deciding to ask), which keeps house rule 2, but the "mystery" never rewards detecting.
- **Ch2–4 have little comedy and Ch3–8 carry a lot of hurt.** Ch2 is all muttering. Ch3, 5, 7 and 8 each end on Reba's shame, rising to "felt worse than she had ever felt in her whole life" (l.313) and "It was the worst thing I've ever done" (l.324).

### Strengths

- **Ch1 world-building.** The Mitten Tree is a believable, timeless folk custom. It sits naturally beside Grandpappy's Bridge, which v1.0 calls "the traditional meeting place for anyone with news to share", so the muttering there later is canon-true. Reba identifying mittens by smell ("This one's been near a… Cinnamon", l.21) is a charming introduction to her investigator nose.
- **"It did not keep everybody warm until spring. It did not even last until the next morning."** (l.41–43). A fine, gentle hook.
- **Reba as suspect (concept flag b) is handled largely well.** Beau's blurt (l.105) is clueless rather than cruel and fits his v1.1 "occasionally clueless". Rusty repeats the mill gossip but immediately owns it: "I shouldn't have repeated it" (l.115). Maggie Mae defends Reba at once and firmly (l.123). "Not everybody knows me" (l.125) is the best line in the chapter.
- **The littlest rabbit at the tree (l.158–166).** The custom working exactly as it should, seen through the stakeout, is a lovely beat. "That's what it's for" (l.164) earns Reba's later reversal.
- **Ch5's turn.** Maggie Mae makes the mirror explicit through evidence, the notebook, rather than through scolding. Reba reaches "I did the same thing" (l.221) herself. That is a real turning point. It needs trimming, not cutting (L1-3).
- **Sadie Belle's "as a neighbour first. Not as a detective."** (l.244). It fits her v1.1 gentle, encouraging character, and it changes how the visit happens. Maggie Mae putting her notebook away (l.246) is quietly perfect.
- **Ch7's reveal is beautifully paced.** Reba's heart sinks at the heap of mittens, then "But then she looked closer" (l.289), and the mittens turn out to be unravelling, not hoarded. The nine tiny pairs counted "without meaning to" (l.295) land.
- **Mrs Quill's explanation of the quills (l.311)** is both a fact lesson and a heartbreak, delivered lightly. "She almost smiled. 'I can't, you know.'"
- **Brody's payoff (l.365–369).** "Nobody ever gets my size right" is warm and true to a bear cub. It pays off the Ch1 cinnamon mitten.
- **The saying fits the story's own words.** #74 directly rebuts the mutterers' "You never know with the quiet ones" (l.193). That is a deft, unforced echo.
- **The close is staged at Mrs Quill's gate, not Buck's porch.** That is fresh staging under the founder's "staging varied" default. Buck's fire is handled impeccably (l.373, l.416), and the invitation to the Longest Night (l.394–402) ties the story to v1.0's "no one is cold or lonely on the hardest night of the year".

### Weaknesses

1. **Permission (concept flag a), Ch2, Ch4, Ch7.** Mrs Quill takes "every one, every night". That includes Maggie Mae's six cupboard pairs, Reba's mother's three "one day" pairs, and the four fresh pairs (l.170). None of these are described as holey. She also takes the small ones, so the first little rabbit goes without (l.58). The custom allows "one", to those who need it (l.5). Nobody, Mrs Quill included, ever acknowledges that she should have said something. The card then retroactively blesses the practice ("bring them to her", l.346). There is also an internal logic gap: why unravel good mittens to make other mittens?
2. **Recycled from S01-007** (see above): the rumour relay (l.191), the public "is very sorry, and says so" (l.346), the everybody-helps montage (Ch9), and Reba's paws finding their use (l.377). It also means two of Reba's three featured stories so far cast her as the source of Hollow-wide misinformation. That is a character-pattern risk for the founder to watch. Canon says she is "never malicious or dishonest by nature".
3. **The moral is stated before the saying (house rule 1).**
   - Maggie Mae, l.205: "And they didn't ask you. They just decided." And l.209: "We didn't ask her why. We just decided. Because she's quiet, and prickly, and keeps to herself." This is the theme, spelled out. The notebook alone makes the point.
   - Reba, l.324: "I decided you were greedy, without asking… somebody decided the same thing about me, without asking… I did exactly the same thing to you." This recaps Ch5 almost word for word.
   - Buck's lead-in, l.408: "I never asked. I'm ashamed to say it, but I never once asked." This paraphrases the lesson immediately before the saying. It also gives Buck an uncharacteristic note of shame (Canon 2: he never lectures; warmth is his register).
   - The card (l.346) and the final sentence ("nobody had to wonder any longer", l.434) restate it again in softer form.
4. **The detective strand is unpaid (Ch3).** The pine-and-old-leaves scent (l.93) is never connected to Mrs Quill. It could be: in winter, porcupines feed on conifer bark and needles. The map is never used. The mystery is solved by luck of timing.
5. **Comedy in Ch2–4 is thin and does not escalate (house rule 4).** The staged comic beats are Reba going cross-eyed (l.93), Beau's "Oh, yes. That makes much more sense" (l.111) and the chickadee on her ear (l.150). Ch2 has none. The sad and guilty sections (Ch3 end, Ch5, Ch7 end, Ch8) total roughly 1,000 words. The two superlatives of shame (l.313, l.324) are too heavy for bedtime, and "worst thing I've ever done" sits oddly beside her fib in 007.
6. **Charlie is missing until Ch8, then appears with a claim the story does not support.** "I ASKED" (l.348): asked whom, and when? She was not at the burrow. For Maggie Mae's canon mentee and best friend in a Maggie Mae story, she is under-used. "Smaller, in large wobbly letters" (l.348) contradicts itself.
7. **Ensemble (house rule 3) is met, but only just.** Beau and Rusty move the plot (by accident), and Sadie Belle shapes the visit. Benny, Barney and Brody relay the rumour and then appear in a list in Ch9 (l.373). Brody is a recipient rather than a contributor. Maverick is absent, which is fine.
8. **Maggie Mae as principal.** The title is hers, but Reba carries the emotional arc and most of the action from Ch3 on. Maggie Mae's contributions (organising, the notebook, the card) are good and in character. Her Southern-belle hostess warmth, though, is barely voiced. One hostess beat would help (she brings the cordial, or sets out the chairs "properly").
9. **Recycled close details (house rule 5).** "The lamps were going out, one after another" (l.432) is near-verbatim S01-001 ("the lamps went out, one after another"), and 002, 004 and 007 also end on lamps going out. "The old beaver with his lantern in front" (l.432) echoes S01-009's closing ("the old beaver with his lantern and the two young ones beside him"). Charlie asleep and carried home recurs from 002 and 005. "The first stars began to show over the bare elder hedge" (l.388) is the 006 beat moved to a new place.
10. **An immemorial custom opens two stories in a row.** 010 opens "an old custom, older than anybody can remember"; 011 opens "Nobody can remember who started it" (l.5). Vary the opening of one of them (v1.0 §10 asks for "Sunrise on the Hollow" grounding).
11. **Stock phrases and slips (house rule 6).**
    - "Reba's eyes went bright" (l.82) and "Her eyes were very bright" (l.197) are the banned "eyes shone" family.
    - "glowing… with her paws held out in front of her like a pair of lanterns" (l.160) is near "lit up like a lamp".
    - "long time / long moment / long while" appears seven times (l.56, 160, 217, 264, 326, 383, 406), leaning on "long silence".
    - "did not say anything" appears at l.186 and l.404 (the "Nobody said anything" family).
    - Clichés: "her heart sank right down into her toes" (l.287), "hot and cold all at once" (l.119), "small voice" twice (l.58, l.221).
    - Clumsy lines: "the rabbits had got none" (l.74) is unclear; "like the bristles of a well-brushed brush" (l.266); "big ones that might fit a bear cub with paws a size too big" (l.293).
    - Only one "the way X does" simile (l.178), which is within the limit.
12. **Brief interpretation.** The brief says mittens disappear "throughout Hickory Hollow". The draft concentrates them on one donation tree. This is arguably a sound safeguarding choice, since it means nobody's home is entered. Flag it for founder confirmation.

### Required revisions (Lock One)

1. **L1-1 Fix the permission problem (concept flag a).** Mrs Quill must not take beyond the custom unasked. Preferred approach: she takes **only the worn-through mittens**, and each time she leaves something in their place, such as a small knot of new wool tied on the twig. Nobody understands it, or snow buries it, and it becomes a clue the children read. The good mittens, and the tiny ones the kits needed, go to real needers in the first hard cold, which keeps the "Eleven pairs in a night" alarm honest. Alternative: an old, half-forgotten part of the custom ("worn ones may be taken by anybody who'll mend them"), which Buck may *remember only after the children have found it* (house rule 2). Either way, cut "It's easier" as the last word on secrecy, and let Mrs Quill own, in one line, that she should have said.
2. **L1-2 De-recycle from S01-007.** Take Reba's apology off the card (l.346). Let her put the rumour right herself, on stage, by walking the chain back in reverse (Barney → Benny → Brody → the rabbit mother → the miller's wife). This can be gently comic, and it also clears her own name (see S3). Keep the knitting-afternoon payoff, but avoid a second "Reba's paws sort the problem" climax in the 007 manner. The unpicking can stay as a quiet grace note rather than the resolution.
3. **L1-3 State the moral once.** Cut Maggie Mae's l.205 "And they didn't ask you. They just decided." and l.209's "We didn't ask her why. We just decided. Because…". Let the two-line notebook page do it. Trim Reba's Ch8 confession to the facts and the apology, without re-explaining the mirror. Cut Buck's l.408 to "I've known Mrs Quill a good many years. Longer than any of you have been alive." with no "never asked" and no "ashamed". Keep #74 verbatim. Reconsider the final sentence's "nobody had to wonder any longer".
4. **L1-4 Stage escalating comedy in Ch2–4 and shorten the hurt.** For example, Reba's nose follows the wrong trail in two or three escalating steps (Brody's cinnamon mitten to Brody's kitchen; a creek-water mitten to the miller's), or Beau and Rusty's own stakeout ends in a heap of snow. Cut about 300 words from the guilt passages. Replace "worse than she had ever felt in her whole life" and "the worst thing I've ever done" with something proportionate, such as "I'm so sorry. I should have asked."
5. **L1-5 Make the detecting pay off.** Connect the pine scent (l.93) to Mrs Quill, since porcupines feed on conifer bark and needles in winter. Use Maggie Mae's map to point to the end of the creek path, so that the children's deduction, not only the stakeout, leads to the door.
6. **L1-6 Fix the counts and the timeline** (C1–C3 in Lock Two).
7. **L1-7 Give Charlie a real role before Ch8,** so that "I ASKED" is true. For example, Charlie comes on the visit and asks Mrs Quill directly about the quills. Fix "smaller, in large wobbly letters" (l.348).
8. **L1-8 Freshen the close.** Replace "the lamps were going out, one after another" (l.432) and "the old beaver with his lantern in front", and avoid a third "Charlie asleep and carried". One distinctive closing image from this story would serve better, such as the knot of wool on the bare twig (if L1-1 is adopted) or the Mitten Tree full again.
9. **L1-9 Clean up the stock phrases and clumsy lines** listed in weakness 11.
10. **L1-10 Give Maggie Mae one principal-sized beat of her own**, in her hostess or organiser voice, in the second half. Vary the opening so that 010 and 011 do not both begin with "older than anybody can remember".

### Facts ("Sherlock")

**F1 Porcupines in the Appalachians. Pass, with a note.** The North American porcupine (*Erethizon dorsatum*) is native to the Appalachians. Its eastern range, however, runs south through Pennsylvania, West Virginia and the mountains of western Virginia and Maryland. It is generally considered absent from the Great Smokies and the North Carolina/Tennessee Blue Ridge, which v1.0 Canon 1 names as the Hollow's inspiration. In a timeless valley that is "never named as a real place" this is acceptable, but the founder should know. No text change is required. Avoid any line claiming porcupines are common "all through these mountains".

**F2 "Cannot throw quills." Correct ✓.** No porcupine can project its quills; this is a well-known myth. "Nobody can" (l.311, l.348) is accurate.

**F3 "Quills only come out if you push against them." Correct in substance ✓.** The quills are loosely anchored and barbed, and they detach on contact. A porcupine may also swing its tail at a threat to drive quills in. That is not throwing, and it is not needed here. Keep the line as is.

**F4 Quills lie flat when calm and rise when alarmed. Correct ✓.** The quills rising slightly at the door and settling when Mrs Quill sees Buck (l.274, l.359) is an accurate and elegant use of real behaviour.

**F5 No hibernation. Correct ✓.** Porcupines stay active all winter, often keeping to a den in bad weather. Her being up and about in deep snow is right. Porcupines are also largely nocturnal or crepuscular and solitary, so the dusk visit, the night deliveries and the lone household are all fact-true. A nice fit.

**F6 Den.** Porcupines usually den in rock crevices, hollow trees, logs or other animals' old burrows rather than digging their own. A "burrow under the elder hedge" is fine in-world ✓.

**F7 Quill colour. Check.** North American porcupine quills are usually described as **pale (whitish or yellowish) with dark tips**. Long guard hairs can look pale-tipped and frosted. "Pale-tipped quills" (l.178) is probably the wrong way round. Suggest "long cream quills with dark tips", or simply "long sharp quills".

**F8 Size.** Adult porcupines (typically about 5–14 kg) are usually larger than raccoons. "Smaller than Reba had expected" (l.266) is relative to expectation and to Reba's dread, so it is acceptable. Do not state that she is smaller than a raccoon.

**F9 Knitting plausibility.** Porcupine forefeet have four long-clawed digits and can grasp food and bark dexterously, and porcupines are expert climbers (standing up to reach the branches, l.170, is plausible). Knitting is anthropomorphic, but within the series' conventions it is as plausible as Buck whittling ✓. Volume: nine thimble-sized pairs plus one large pair in about three days is possible for a skilled knitter working long evenings. It is *not* consistent with "all week" (C2).

**F10 Unravelling and re-knitting wool. Correct, with one nuance ✓.** Unravelling ("unpicking" or "frogging") a hand-knit and re-knitting the yarn is a real, traditional thrift practice. Reclaimed yarn is kinked ("crinkly", l.291) ✓ and is usually soaked or steamed to relax it. Worn-through mittens break the yarn at the holes, so they give **shorter lengths that have to be joined**, and palms that have felted from wear may not unravel at all. The miller's wife's odd ends of spare wool (l.363) neatly solve the shortfall. One optional clause ("and knot the short bits together") would make it exact. Unravelling normally starts from the cast-off edge, where the end is woven in. "Found the end of the wool" (l.291) is fine.

**F11 Raccoon senses.** Raccoons have a keen sense of smell ✓, and they are especially famous for highly sensitive, dexterous forepaws. That makes Reba's gift for unpicking (l.377) a fact-true character beat ✓.

**F12 Other natural history.** Flowering dogwood is native, small and bare in winter ✓. American elderberry is native ✓, but "elder hedge" is a British hedgerow idiom; "elderberry thicket" would suit the Appalachian atmosphere better (001 uses "the elderberry"). American holly ✓. A chickadee landing on a perfectly still animal (l.150) is plausible for a bold Carolina chickadee ✓. Pine scent ✓; porcupines eat conifer bark in winter (useful for L1-5).

**F13 Fire.** A chimney "poking up through" a tangle of elder (l.256) would be a fire risk in reality. In-world it is acceptable. Optionally say "up beside the hedge". Buck's outdoor fire "well away from the hedge", kept by him and put out with snow (l.373, l.416), is exemplary ✓.

### Facts ("Sherlock")

**F1 Virginia opossum (*Didelphis virginiana*) in the Smokies. Correct ✓.** It is native and common throughout the southern Appalachians. Its range has also historically expanded northward, so "from further south" is a nice true touch. It is nocturnal ✓ (l.214). It is solitary and uses existing dens rather than digging ✓ ("took the empty hollow", l.315). It does not hibernate, though it is less active in hard cold ✓.

**F2 Playing dead (thanatosis). Mostly correct; one fix (L2-7).** It is involuntary, a response to fright ("Can't help it. Just happens to them") ✓. The animal falls on its side ✓ with mouth open and tongue out ✓, and recovers when the threat has gone ✓, after minutes to hours. But an opossum playing dead is usually **limp and curled, with eyes open or half-closed**, not "stiff as a stick, eyes shut". It also drools and gives off a foul-smelling fluid. Leaving the smell out is right for this series, especially with a skunk lead.

**F3 Hissing and teeth. Correct ✓.** An open-mouthed hiss and bared teeth are the opossum's usual first bluff. "A great many small white teeth" is right: 50, the most of any North American land mammal.

**F4 Tracks. Correct ✓.** Front feet show five spread, hand-like toes ✓. The hind foot has an opposable, clawless "thumb" (hallux) that sticks out sideways ✓ (l.192, l.210). The hind foot often lands near or on the front print ✓ (l.198), and a tail-drag line in snow is a recognised sign ✓ (l.202). Size "bigger than [a chipmunk's] paw and smaller than [a raccoon's]" ✓. Front tracks are roughly 4–5 cm, against 6–8 cm for a raccoon.

**F5 Carrying bundles in the prehensile tail. Correct ✓.** Opossums carry nesting material (leaves, grass) in a loop of the tail. Using the tail for grip while climbing is also correct ✓ (l.239). The text wisely does not claim that adults hang by the tail, which is a myth. Four pairs of mittens is a generous bundle but a plausible exaggeration.

**F6 Appearance. Correct ✓.** Grey, long pale pointed face, small rounded black ears, long bare pinkish tail, pink feet, black eyes, pink nose. Very soft grey underfur ✓ (l.100). "A little bigger than Reba" (l.239) is plausible for a young raccoon; adult opossums are generally smaller than adult raccoons.

**F7 Persimmons. Correct ✓.** The opossum's love of ripe persimmons is both real and classic Appalachian folklore. It is the perfect scent clue, but it is not paid off (see Lock One).

**F8 Wool. Correct ✓.** "Holes as big as a paw. You can't darn a hole that size" (l.372) is a sensible reason to unravel rather than mend. Knotting short pieces together (l.366) fixes the round-1 nuance ✓. Reclaimed yarn is "crinkly" ✓.

**F9 Lifespan.** Wild opossums usually live only two to four years, so "Granny" is in-world licence. That is fine for an anthropomorphic Hollow; no change needed.

**F10 Other natural history.** The elderberry thicket along the creek ✓ (r1 fix). Shrivelled berries clinging in winter is plausible ✓. The dogwood ✓, the chickadee ✓, the creek steaming at dawn in hard cold ✓.
