## LOCK ONE: "Piers Morgan" (story quality)

**Score: 7.9 / 10. FAIL (pass mark 8.5)**

### Strengths
- **The comedy engine is the best in recent S01.** The plan grows on stage in Ch2: troughs, then tower, then counterweight, then lever, then gate, then two bells, then a wheel and flag (l.60–74). Barney's "Brilliant" fades each time (l.62, l.66), which is a clean and readable comic device.
- **Each test fails bigger than the last.** Charlie is soaked (l.107), Barney is soaked under the tipping bucket (l.152–158), and then comes "Ting. CLANG" and the bees (l.213–215). This is genuine staged escalation (Rule 4).
- **Benny's counting is a strong spine for his character.** The 42 trips, the 588-trip build week (l.86, which correctly calls out how the machine costs more than it saves) and "One bucket makes one cupful" (l.168) are all good.
- **Benny is on canon.** The Bible gives his flaw as taking himself and his work too seriously and needing his brother's silliness. The story dramatises exactly that.
- **The new Ch6 resistance beat works.** "Anybody didn't." / "Anybody didn't. Except you." (l.268–276) gives Benny a real change of heart instead of instant agreement. It is the best addition in the expansion.
- **Barney's idea is deliberate and observed, as the coordinator's variety flag asked.** The barrel is planted early in Ch2 (l.88, the miller rolling a barrel). Barney's watching is shown (l.230, l.234) and his visit to Granny Opal is shown.
- **Ch8 is warm and funny.** The brothers laugh in the dark (l.322–354), and the turn from shame to laughter is earned.
- **Buck's saying is word for word.** #32, "Build it right the first time and you'll have time to sit on the porch." (l.389), matches Bible v1.0 §8 exactly. The Ch10 "Sit on the porch" payoff (l.408) makes a loosely fitting saying land.

### Weaknesses
1. **The counting spine contradicts itself.** Benny's arithmetic is the backbone of the story, so this matters.
   - "Trips" means cans of water. Moving the water source to the bed shortens each trip but does not reduce the number of cans needed.
   - Straw put down that same evening on dry soil cannot cut one soaking from 42 cans to 6 (l.313–317).
   - Granny Opal's point is watering once a day (l.283). Yet Ch10 assumes six cans twice a day: Charlie says "thirty-six trips a day left over" and Benny corrects her to "Seventy-two… Thirty-six times two" (l.398–402). Under the story's own logic the saving is 84 − 6 = 78.
   - Benny, the careful counter, gets his own lesson's arithmetic wrong.
2. **The moral is signposted on both sides of Buck (Rule 1).**
   - Before Buck: the new line from the miller's wife, "I never thought of it as clever. I just thought of it as easier." (l.379), comes two lines before Buck and states the theme as a maxim. Benny's "I was too busy drawing bells to see it" (l.385) adds a second summary.
   - After Buck: "Something simple… Something very, very simple." (l.418) restates the moral.
   - Earlier in the story, theme-adjacent lines pile up: l.191 (Sadie Belle), l.240 and l.297 ("not very clever", said twice by Barney), l.266, l.268 and l.301.
3. **The close is built from earlier closes.** Each beat is individually small, but together they read as a medley.
   - The relapse joke "Possibly with a little bell on it." / "NO BELLS" (l.422–424) has the same structure as 009's "No palaces." / "But maybe a very small heap." That was the same brothers in their last close together.
   - The bell "went ting, once, far away" (l.410) echoes 002's closing "Ting." from a brass bell heard far off.
   - The hummingbird at the red trumpets on Buck's rail, "and then it was gone" (l.410), re-runs 019's closing image.
   - The final image is an object waiting quietly in the dark for the next rain (l.426). This is close cousin to the reviewers' "waiting for next year" and object-at-night closes (002, 014, 022).
   - Benny keeps the wheel for "what I'm going to invent next" (l.367–371, l.412–414), which also leans towards the "kept for next time" pattern.
4. **The ensemble is mostly props and commentary (Rule 3).**
   - The adults do most of the real work: the miller (troughs, stone, bees, barrel), Brody's father (saw, tower, barrel) and the farmer (cowbell, straw).
   - Among the friends:
     - Maggie Mae lends things, and the bell is the climax trigger, so that counts.
     - Brody lifts the stone, which is also a safety problem (see Lock Three).
     - Maverick only reports on the bees.
     - Reba (paper bag, lemonade) and Charlie (gets wet, holds an umbrella, asks the Ch10 question) are decoration.
     - Rusty and Sadie Belle each get one line in Ch5. Rusty's "wait for it to rain" (l.191) is a nice seed that is never paid off.
   - Rule 3 needs at least three friends whose contribution moves the plot. Only Maggie Mae clearly qualifies.
5. **The story is too close to 015 (the third Benny/Barney pairing).**
   - The resolution is still that Barney's covering idea is right. In 015 it was a blanket over the bucket, with straw on boards as the lid. Here it is straw over the soil, called "like a blanket" (l.287) and "a deep, soft, golden blanket" (l.311), with "tuck it in" (l.287, l.311).
   - The deliberate observation (barrel and Granny Opal) does differentiate the story. But the repeated blanket and straw wording invites the comparison.
   - The barrel should carry the resolution, and the straw should not be described as a blanket.
6. **Barney is under-voiced.** Canon Barney is a free spirit who turns chores into games, splashes and is heedless. Here he is shy and apologetic: "not very clever" three times (l.240, l.297), plus "You'll probably think it's silly". Only the Ch1 run idea (l.21) has his energy. The barrel discovery could come through play (splashing at the barrel's tap, racing raindrops down the Mill gutter) and still be deliberate.
7. **Continuity problems weaken the turning point.** See Lock Two: the cottage's own water butt from 022, the single occupied bee gum, and the geometry of the bean bed against the cottage.

### Sherlock: verdict REVISE

**Required fixes**
- **S-1. Counterweight physics (l.138–148).** The miller says the right weight is "about half a bucket of water".
  - A counterweight lighter than the full bucket cannot raise it "by itself" when the lever releases. With half a bucket, the full bucket stays down, as Benny himself says at l.138.
  - The stone is then "much heavier than Benny had expected" (l.142), which would work, but the miller's stated rule is wrong.
  - Fix: the miller says the stone must be a little heavier than a full bucket. It lifts the full bucket, but then somebody has to pull the empty bucket back down every time. This is correct, and it adds a comic layer of "more work".
- **S-2. Yellowed leaves do not turn green again (l.356).** "with the yellow gone from their edges" is wrong. Drought-yellowed tissue does not recover. Use: "the yellow leaves no worse, the drooping gone, and new green leaves and red flowers opening".
- **S-3. Order of watering and mulching.** Good practice is to soak dry soil first and then mulch. Watering through a thick fresh straw layer partly sheds and absorbs the water. Granny Opal would know this. Reorder l.311–313 so the brothers water from the barrel first and then spread the straw. This also helps fix the arithmetic.
- **S-4. Trip arithmetic.** See L1-1. Mulch reduces evaporation and how often you water. It does not reduce the water needed to soak dry soil on day one.
- **S-5. Bees and noise (l.215).** Honeybees barely detect airborne sound. They react strongly to vibration and jolts.
  - A single cowbell clang at the far wall pulling out the whole colony is a stretch. The trough line runs round the outer path and the gums are at the hub.
  - Fix: the cowbell swings and bangs against the trough and its stones, jolting the line and the commotion near the hub, and "dozens of bees" (from the one gum) come out crossly. Keep "Nobody was stung" and the miller's calm handling, which is correct practice.
- **S-6. Open water butts.** An open barrel in August breeds mosquito larvae (022's butt already had tadpoles). Add a board lid with a gap for the downpipe. This is also a safety fix (see Lock Three).

## LOCK THREE: "Dr Noura" (child safety, wellbeing, bedtime suitability) and narration readiness

### Dr Noura: verdict PASS WITH CONDITIONS

**Conditions (required)**
- **N-1.** Brody, a cub, must not lift the heavy counterweight stone (l.142). The grown-ups lift it, as in M-5.
- **N-2.** A suspended heavy stone with a release lever is machinery (Rule 7).
  - At l.148 and l.203 a child pulls the lever while children stand nearby. At l.152 Barney is standing directly under the hoist.
  - Add one clause: Brody's father keeps a paw on the lever, or pulls it, and everyone stands well back from both sides of the tower, "as the miller had told them".
  - Barney's soaking can still happen if he steps in to look up the spout.
  - Children may copy backyard pulley-and-stone contraptions, so the adults-only framing matters.
- **N-3.** Water butts and open barrels are a real drowning risk for small children.
  - Give both barrels a fitted board lid (see S-6).
  - Have Brody's father fit or set the barrel (already partly done at l.309).
  - Keep the children's part to turning the low tap (l.313), which is safe.
- **N-4 (advisory).** Barney digs clay "from the creek bank" (l.113). Add "with Brody's father nearby" or "at the dry edge of the bank".
