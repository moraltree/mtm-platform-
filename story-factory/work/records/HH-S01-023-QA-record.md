## Lock One

### "Piers Morgan", senior editor and literary critic

### Score: 7.6 / 10. NOT PASSED (pass mark 8.5)

The engine is excellent. Chapters 2–4 are the most precisely escalated appetite comedy in the season:
- "That one was just to test";
- the "One for the basket. One for Reba. One for Reba." rhythm (l.60);
- the dip, the piling-up, the rhubarb-leaf false bottom ("a bowl of blackberries with something hiding underneath it. Which it was", l.170);
- the walnut-sized last berry;
- the stool going one way and Reba the other.

The squirrel fib, undone by the latch Reba herself did up, is a genuinely clever piece of plotting for this age group. The self-control turn at l.319–321 ("a whisker away from her nose … through her teeth") is real, physical and funny.

It is not near 8.5, for one dominant reason and several cheap ones. **The draft is, beat for beat, 007 again.** The founder flagged exactly this, and the draft also re-plays 003's heirloom resolution almost verbatim. The appetite engine was meant to be kept, and it is; the *scaffolding around it* was meant to change, and it hasn't. Beyond that:
- the close recycles 007's walk-home-with-Mama and its food-quantity punchline, and lands on a Reba-food image, a cousin of the banned "Reba's crumb";
- there are three safety breaches (a child with a knife, children filling a washtub, a wasp waved at);
- the chapter order puts Reba in two places at once (Ch8/Ch9);
- the "catch it while it's fresh" stain lore is applied about six hours after the spill;
- Maggie Mae's purple kitchen is never cleaned, so the "making amends" half of the moral skips the most obvious amend.

### Strengths

- **On-stage escalation, properly costed (Ch2–4).** Each step is a bigger cheat than the last: an excuse (l.50), a pattern (l.60), concealment by rearranging (l.154), concealment by fraud (the rhubarb, l.166), and the overreach that causes the disaster (l.174–190). A child can follow every rung.
- **The fib is fair play.** The latched window is planted innocently as a triumph ("Guarded", l.119–121) and pays off as the giveaway (l.223–225). "Reba looked at the window too. And then her face crumpled" is the best two-line moment in the draft. The retraction is complete, and Reba names the fib as "the worst bit" in her own words.
- **The self-control aids are a fresh ensemble mechanism.** Charlie's chant, Benny's audible count ("much harder to eat a berry when everybody would hear the number stop", l.309), and Barney's anti-help, with Sadie Belle removing him. These are tools *for* Reba's weakness, chosen by children, and they are new to the series. Maverick spots, Brody reaches high, Rusty finds the unpicked shady patch, Beau runs water. Everybody does real work.
- **Turning point earned and wordless** (l.319–323). "Everybody cheered" is the right size.
- **Benny's number as a payoff.** "Eight hundred and forty-three" (l.330) returns in Maggie Mae's mouth (l.397). It is good for read-aloud.
- **Ch10's washing-up** (l.414–430) is new staging for S01: bubbles into the rafters, everybody purple, Sadie Belle's purple nose "that nobody had dared to mention", and "I was purple first" is a lovely line.
- **Wisdom fit.** #92 is the exact saying for this story, delivered cleanly, in a varied (supper-table) staging the founder allows.

### "Sherlock", facts and logic

**Verdict: PASS WITH REQUIRED FIXES.**

**Facts checked:**
- **Blackberry season, southern Appalachians: correct.** Wild blackberries (*Rubus* spp., e.g. Allegheny blackberry) ripen from late June into August, depending on elevation. Mid-July is peak at mid elevations, so "the middle of July" (l.11) and a heavily laden bank are accurate, and match the canon's "blackberries ripen mid-summer". "Her first blackberry of the year" (l.39) is fine for Reba personally; lower slopes may have had a few earlier.
- **Wasps: broadly correct, but the handling model needs a fix.** Social wasps (yellowjackets) are common around ripe and damaged fruit from midsummer, so "there were already wasps" (l.13) is right, and a wasp nosing at a cloth over berries is plausible. "You never swat a wasp" is sound advice: swatting provokes stings, and a crushed wasp can release alarm scent. But standard advice is also *not to wave or flap* at a wasp; stay calm, keep still or move slowly away, and give it an open way out. Waving a tea towel in loops, however gently, models flapping (N-3, R-6). "Sniffing" is acceptable figurative shorthand (wasps smell with their antennae).
- **Bees "bumbling drunkenly from berry to berry" (l.13): inaccurate as stated, and off-tone.** Bees forage mainly on bramble *flowers*. Some late flowers do persist in July, and bees only occasionally take juice from split or overripe fruit; it is wasps that work the fruit. "Drunkenly" (and "a little bit winey", same line) introduces intoxication imagery for a bedtime audience. Use "bees bumbling among the last of the white flowers".
- **Stain removal: method correct, timing wrong.**
  - Cold water first is right. Berry juice (anthocyanin pigment) is best flushed out promptly with cold water, pressing through from the back without rubbing.
  - Hot water and heat can set many fruit stains. "Sets it in for ever" overclaims (an old boiling-water method for *fresh* fruit stains does exist), but it is acceptable as grandmother-lore if softened to "can set it in".
  - Lemon juice plus sun on white cotton or linen is a genuine traditional bleaching method. The colour shift to *pink* is also correct chemistry: anthocyanins redden in acid.
  - **However**, the draft's own premise is "comes out if you catch it while it's fresh" (l.383), and the cloth sits stained on the floor from mid-morning until after half past two (l.328, l.375). That is about five or six hours. Fix: Maggie Mae drops the cloth into a basin of cold water to soak *straight after the spill* (Ch6, a natural "first thing" for an organised hostess), and the lemon and sun come in Ch9. This is truer and fixes M-3 at the same time (R-7).
  - One hour of lemon and sun fading the stain to "a soft, faint, rosy blush" is plausible.
- **Rhubarb leaves (l.166–170): fix.** Rhubarb leaves are poisonous (oxalic acid). Stuffing them among food that is then partly salvaged for pies (l.246–248: "the ones on the table were all right") is a bad thing to model, and an attentive parent will object. Use cabbage or lettuce leaves from the garden (just as funny: "cabbage leaves on her head"), or a folded tea towel.
- **"The size of a walnut" (l.174).** Wild blackberries run about 1–2.5 cm; a walnut in its shell is 3–4 cm. As Reba-eyed hyperbole it is borderline. "The size of a thimble" or "as big as a robin's egg" would be truer. Advisory.
- **Butter logic (l.101).** If the pastry is made, the trip for butter needs another purpose: "pastry for six, and butter for the other six", or fetch sugar instead.
- **Berry arithmetic: sound.**
  - Twelve pies at four baskets is three per basket. Four pies are saved; eight remain, which is "three baskets, at least" (l.264). Consistent.
  - 843 berries in about three and a half hours is roughly four a minute; plausible for one picker.
  - Nit: Brody drops *his* high berries into Reba's basket (l.305), so Benny's 843 is not all Reba's picking. Maggie Mae's "you picked eight hundred and forty-three" (l.397) is generous but harmless, or say "your basket had".
- **The last berry "rolled off the tray" at the Mill (l.362).** The pies were assembled at Rose Arch, so loose berries would not be on the trays. This goes if R-1 removes the scene.
- **Lemons in the Hollow.** Precedent: Maggie Mae's lemonade (018). Acceptable; noted for the founder.

## Lock Three

### "Dr Noura", child development, safety and emotional tone

**Verdict: NOT PASSED** (three cheap safety fixes; the emotional arc itself is healthy).

**What works for a 3–7 audience**
- The lie is small, panicked and instantly undone. The child hears that the fib was "the worst bit" in Reba's own voice, and that the truth is met with thanks, not punishment (l.229–235). That is exactly the right modelling.
- The self-control turn is concrete and imitable: a chant, a count, friends who help you resist. These are real strategies young children use (external scaffolds for impulse control), dramatised warmly.
- The guilt is acknowledged, not wallowed in. Reba's distress (l.229, l.252) is resolved within a page by a plan.
- Asking for help, knocking on doors and saying "it's my fault" (l.283) models taking responsibility in public, gently.

**Required**
- **N-1 (blade).** Charlie, guided by Maggie Mae's paw, uses a knife point to make the leaf veins (l.344). Maggie Mae is a young skunk, so this is a child guiding a child with a blade (rule 7). Fix: Maggie Mae (or better, a grown-up) cuts the leaves; Charlie presses the veins with the tines of a fork or the end of a wooden skewer. This keeps the craft moment.
- **N-2 (water tub).** "They filled the old tin washtub at the pump" (l.385): rule 7 lists water tubs among grown-up jobs. Fix: use a basin in the sink (and move the cold soak to Ch6; R-7), or have Reba's mother fill and carry the tub.
- **N-3 (wasp).** Replace the towel-waving (l.119) with keeping very still, then opening the window wide and waiting, and shutting it when the wasp has gone. The window-latch clue survives intact. The "wave goodbye to somebody on a boat" simile can migrate to Reba's relieved wave *after* it leaves, keeping the one "the way" simile.

**Advisory**
- "Never in her life been so frightened of anything as she was of the look on Maggie Mae's face" (l.213) makes a friend's face the most frightening thing in Reba's life. Use "so worried" or "so afraid of what Maggie Mae would say".
- Barney's "Mmmmm" teasing (l.311) is at Reba's expense (Bible §15), though the narrator disowns it and Sadie Belle removes him. Acceptable as written. It would be warmer if Barney's "something else to do" pays off, for example by keeping the water cups filled.
- "I didn't say you had the biggest tummy" (l.72) is gentle and in character. Keep, but it is the limit.
- Rhubarb leaves near food (Sherlock): change for safety modelling as well as fact.
