## Lock One — "Piers Morgan"

**Score: 8.0 / 10. Below the 8.5 pass mark. REVISE.**

### Strengths
- **The premise is genuinely original and the moral is dramatised, not stated.** Charlie supplies the wonder, Maverick the facts. Their exchange ("It's water." / "It's water shaped like a castle.", l.31–33) sets up the theme in two lines. Maverick's arc from literal-minded to inventive ("I made it up... Out of my own head", l.166) is a real arc that fits his character, and so is his reflex to puff up his feathers and then let them down again (l.94, l.211).
- **Science and imagination support each other.** Each cloud type becomes a chapter beat. Maverick's description of being inside stratus is what unsticks the story (l.195–209), and his reading of the anvil is what saves everyone (l.238–248). This is the best fit of secondary theme to plot in recent S01 drafts.
- **The 014 overlap is handled.** The "story written by the sky, one day's clouds per chapter" device (l.117–121) is clearly different from 014's Story Night. The exception is the round-robin retelling in Ch 9 (see weaknesses).
- **The climax is earned and led by the children.** Maverick takes charge, Benny counts from flash to bang, Brody comforts the littlest rabbit, Rusty and Beau warn the Hollow. Recasting the giant from "coming to fight" to "coming to help" (l.287) is a lovely beat that the payoff answers: the Princess comes down as rain (l.365) and the Wisp is no longer lonely (l.371).
- **Strong individual lines.** Brody's "He's just clearing his throat" (l.258). Rusty's "No. But he gets a lovely view" (l.148). Barney's "He can't row" (l.353). Maggie Mae's "he's doing a very good job" (l.316).
- **Mountain Wisdom handled correctly.** #109 is quoted word for word (l.377), with no summarising lead-in and no restatement afterwards.

### Weaknesses
1. **The close is recycled (house rule 5).** Ch 10 repeats the shape and much of the wording of recent closes:
   - "grown-ups ... along the creek path with their lanterns ... the porch began to empty, a family at a time, with goodnights all round" (l.398) nearly copies 019 ("the porch emptied, a few at a time, with goodnights all round").
   - "went home on her father's shoulders" (l.398) copies 012.
   - Charlie walking home beside her mother's lantern to "the round green door under the big beech", pausing on the step and going in to bed (l.408–426) is 013's close almost beat for beat. Her mother's lantern is also in 022.
   - "the lamp just lit beside the door" (l.375) and the honeysuckle smell (l.410) both repeat 021.
   - 019, 021, 022 and 023 all end with lantern-led walks home. A fifth in a row is not a "fresh close".
2. **It contradicts 010.** In 010 l.160 Buck rules: "no more flying into cloud. And no flying after dark". Here Maverick says "I fly through them sometimes" (l.25) and "When I fly through stratus..." (l.195) as a current habit. His "once I flew into one by mistake ... a crow laughed at me" (l.25) also retells 010's incident in a different and lighter version. In 010 he flew in at dusk, alone, landed on the miller's woodshed and frightened the Hollow. Buck's rule must hold.
3. **The comedy is told in summary, not escalated on stage (rule 4).** Ch 4's best material, the bear who cannot row, the fox losing its legs, the pie, Beau's leap falling into three pieces and Maggie Mae's abandoned peas, is packed into two long summary paragraphs (l.138, l.150). Ch 2 is gentle and wistful rather than funny. The comedy builds, but mostly offstage.
4. **The Ch 9 round-robin echoes 014.** "Benny told about... Barney told about... Rusty told about..." (l.357), with Buck listening, recreates 014's "every youngster, one story each, I will listen" staging. That is the very overlap the coordinator differentiated against.
5. **Staging errors in Ch 10.**
   - Maverick is already "beside her on the rail" (l.390) yet later "hopped along the rail until he was beside her" (l.402).
   - The grown-ups' lanterns are "coming ... now" (l.398), but Maverick "could not stay till the lanterns came" (l.402).
   - Charlie's mother "came" twice (l.400, l.408).
6. **Maverick's "old hollow in my hickory ... outside his door" (l.283, l.302) is new canon, wrong biology, and against earlier canon.** In 004 and 011 he roosts on a branch. It also undermines storm safety (see Sherlock and Dr Noura).
7. **Small slips.**
   - Charlie says "Spoiling" and Maverick answers "It isn't ruining it" (l.191/195).
   - Maverick says "Not running" and Rusty sets off "at his fastest" (l.277/279).
   - The narration pre-empts the mackerel image (l.109) that Maverick then explains (l.111).
8. **The "whole Hollow" part of the brief is only partly delivered.** The grown-ups appear only as hearers and lantern-carriers. Buck's "hearing bits of it all week, all over the Hollow" (l.351) carries the claim almost alone.
9. **The fit of #109 to the moral is acceptable but indirect.** It is the only story-centred saying in §8, so the choice is right. However, "a warm fire" sits against a hot July evening with only a lamp lit. The narration should not draw attention to the missing fire; do not add a fire.

## Lock One — "Piers Morgan"

**Score: 8.8 / 10. PASS (pass mark 8.5), subject to the minor edits R-1 to R-4.**

### Strengths
- **The new close is the best in recent S01.** A real, accurate natural moment (high cirrus lit after the valley is in shadow) ties off the story's own mythology. "He gets the sun last, because he's highest of all" (l.454) turns the lonely Wisp of Ch 2 into "the one who says goodnight to everybody" (l.458). It is drowsy and specific, and it does not depend on objects, inscriptions or a "next year" promise.
- **Ch 4 now works as staged comedy.** The pattern is claim, counter-claim, then the cloud falling apart, and it builds from Benny/Barney to Rusty to Reba/Maverick to Beau. Maverick's "Clouds aren't pies" followed by "the most pie-shaped cumulus I have ever seen" (l.168–172) is the best comic beat in the book, and it serves his arc.
- **The safety handling is now a strength.** Maverick models the rule in his own voice ("not even a magnificent eagle", l.317). The grown-ups visibly take over once warned (l.327), so the children lead and the adults keep things safe, as house rule 2 intends.
- **Canon is honoured with a light touch.** Buck's 010 rules are quoted as Maverick's own discipline rather than a lecture.
- **The theme is dramatised throughout and stated only by #109** (l.415, word for word).

### Weaknesses (minor)
- **R-1:** "with his head tucked down" (l.438) is close to the banned beat "a head tucked under a wing" (rule 5).
- **R-2:** "the first star came out right beside it" (l.460) is not the banned "first star over Sunrise Ridge", since it is in the west. However, it is the same family of beat, and stars close 001, 006 and 010. The ending works without it.
- **R-3:** "the strongest of the field hands" (l.327). In a Southern Appalachian setting, "field hands" carries historical connotations best avoided in a children's franchise. Also, Beau is "sat ... by the stove" on "the hottest day yet" (l.327), which is an odd image.
- **R-4:** In the stratus memory, Maverick is sitting on a rock yet says "not which way is up" (l.229). That line made sense for flying, but not for sitting.
- "A warm fire" in #109 against a summer evening is unchanged. It is acceptable because it is proverbial, and the narration rightly does not draw attention to it.
- The "whole Hollow" part of the brief is now adequately met, through the grown-ups' role in Ch 7 and Buck's "all over the Hollow" (l.389).
