## Lock One

### "Piers Morgan", senior editor and literary critic

### Score: 8.2 / 10. NOT PASSED (pass mark 8.5)

This is one of the strongest premises in the spring run, and the best Maverick story so far. The comic engine is clean and child-legible: four things become "Saturday, meadow, noon, trees, low pass", then "kettles", then twenty-three kettles. The turning point is real (l.164–188). The arc is earned: Maverick goes from "Eagles do not need notes" to sitting still in the fog. Charlie's "send something back" is the best small-friend contribution in several stories.

It falls short of the mark for six reasons:
- **the plot's second message item contradicts canon:** the Great Barn stands *in* Fern Meadow, and the Gathering is held in the meadow;
- **the close recycles 004's ending** (Maverick goes up to his hickory with a keepsake) and **003's wording;**
- **the lesson is recapped after the wisdom,** in Ch10's hare and Benny exchanges;
- **the guilt material is long;**
- **there are three safety slips:** a lit lamp, hot kettles, and an overnight trek offered by two youngsters;
- **there is a counting error** at the comic climax.

### Strengths

- **The message device is pure picture-book clarity.** A four-item list, said aloud as a mantra (l.31, l.56, l.345, l.415), lets a child at bedtime track exactly what is lost and what is restored. The degraded mantra at l.74–78 ("Something about trees") is lovely, funny and true.
- **The comedy escalates on stage and pays off three times:**
  - Ch2: the loop-for-loop rivalry with the young eagle escalates in clear steps (l.70);
  - Ch3: the kettle improvisation runs from "Your… kettles" to "How many kettles?" to "All of them" to "A great deal of dancing" (l.109–121). It is the funniest sequence in the S01 spring stories, and it is entirely in Maverick's character, as brash, kind and bluffing;
  - the payoffs follow: Barney's tea-cosy rehearsal (l.280–296), "Twenty-three kettles! Is it enough, do you think?" (l.356), and the guests bringing them anyway (l.421, l.432).
- **The turning point is real and quietly staged.** The "very long pause" on the porch, Buck putting down his mug ("Where the rockslide was", l.182) and Reba's biscuit stopping halfway (l.184) all land the stakes without any menace.
- **The second turning point is better still.** In Ch7 the fog test is a responsibility choice the child can see. Maverick spreads his wings, stops, and folds them (l.309–319). He also refuses to let Rusty take over (l.329). That is responsibility dramatised, not explained.
- **Benny is exactly right.** He writes it down "just in case" (l.45), asks for the read-back without accusing (l.156–160), and sits at the foot of the tree and waits ("Go away." "No.", l.197–199). He is a careful planner, a touch serious, and kind. Benny saying he "couldn't have done that bit" (l.458) is a warm reversal of the 014 dynamic, though it sits in the wrong place (see R-4).
- **The ensemble contributes in character:**
  - Rusty, the path expert, draws the map (l.250);
  - Maggie Mae, the organised hostess, ties the card on properly (l.252);
  - Charlie's "send something back" comes with a lovely, specific reason, the miller's ticket (l.264–272);
  - Barney turns rehearsal into a game and tests Maverick with deliberate mistakes (l.280–296), which is exactly his v1.0 character.
- **The fiddle-string token is a fresh, non-written proof of receipt.** It also avoids the "inscription" peak that recent reviewers flagged, at least for the token itself.
- **The old badger is a warm new presence,** and his "Good job you came back" (l.386) is generous rather than scolding.

### Weaknesses

1. **The central plot item contradicts canon (major; Moneypenny R-1).** Buck's item two is "It's in Fern Meadow, not the Great Barn, as it used to be" (l.29). The Bible places the Great Barn in Fern Meadow ("In Fern Meadow stands the Great Barn", Bible l.536). It also holds the Blossom Gathering in the meadow ("Fern Meadow fills with flowers and garlands", Bible l.886). So the confusion carries no real stakes, since guests at the Barn are already in the meadow, and "as it used to be" and "It's where the Gathering always is" (l.176) rewrite §6.
2. **The close recycles earlier endings (major; house rule 5 and the freshness brief).** l.464–468 has Maverick fly up to his hickory, settle, look over the evening Hollow and keep a token near him.
   - That is the 004 close: Maverick "flew up to his tall hickory, settled on his branch, and looked down over the whole sleeping valley … tucked the golden feather a little deeper into his pocket".
   - "so that he would see it every day" (l.468) echoes 003's last line almost word for word ("where she would see it every morning").
   - A kept-token close has also appeared in 005, 011, 014 and 017. This is exactly the "kept for next time" family that reviewers asked S01 closes to avoid.
   - l.462, "for longer than anybody could remember", also echoes 016's close ("for longer than anybody in Hickory Hollow could remember").
3. **The lesson is restated after the wisdom (house rule 1).** Ch10 l.442–458 recaps Maverick's virtues twice:
   - the hare asks "Why did you wait?" and Maverick answers "Because of the high path … because I wanted to get there" (l.446–450);
   - Benny then lists them: "It was your flight. And your waiting in the fog. And your saying sorry, twice." (l.458).
   - Benny's notebook tally, "That's a good message" (l.454), is a third summary, and another *written* beat at the peak of the close.
4. **The theme is stated before the wisdom too (house rule 1, minor).**
   - Benny at l.260: "That's how you know they've really got it. Not when you've said it. When they've said it back." This is an aphorism on communication, not just an instruction.
   - The badger at l.390: "Plenty wouldn't have. Plenty would have kept quiet, and hoped." This is an adult giving moral commentary on responsibility.
   - Both pre-empt Buck.
5. **The guilt and shame material is not short (house rule 4).**
   - Ch5 is almost entirely drooping and confession (l.193–229).
   - Ch7 adds "the hardest thing he had done all week. Harder than saying sorry" (l.321).
   - Ch8 repeats the apology (l.362), and Ch10 recounts it (l.458).
   - Maverick apologises fully three times. Each apology is good, but together they are heavy for bedtime.
6. **There are safety slips (house rule 7):**
   - Maggie Mae, a youngster, steadies a lit lamp after Maverick's flapping makes the flame flicker (l.292);
   - Reba pours from twenty-three kettles of hot tea (l.440);
   - Rusty and Beau offer to set off alone on a walk of more than a day, arriving "by tomorrow night" (l.325), and no adult says anything. The offer is declined, but it is modelled as a reasonable plan.
7. **The count at the comic climax does not add up (house rule 9).** Every family brought a kettle, "Some had brought two", making 23 (l.354). On Saturday every family carries one tree and one kettle (l.432). Yet there are 23 trees, "one for every kettle" (l.438, l.454). If some families brought two kettles, there are fewer than 23 families, so fewer than 23 trees.
8. **There is a second untruth (house rule 11).** The confabulation to the badger (l.103–135) is the one permitted untruth, and it is properly confessed to Buck (l.225). However, "Everything … All four things. Like you said." (l.158) is a second claim that Maverick half-knows is false ("only very slightly uneasy … in a place he did not look at", l.139).
9. **Buck asks two questions (house rule 2, strict reading):** "They've got it right?" (l.413) and "And the kettles?" (l.417).
10. **There are banned-phrase near-variants (house rule 6):**
    - "There was a pause … It was a very long pause." (l.166–168) is a synonym swap for "long silence";
    - the badger wears the blossom crown "over one ear" (l.440), a near-variant of the banned "bow over one ear".
    - "anybody could remember" appears four times (l.5, l.188, l.436, l.462).
11. **The seasonal rationale conflicts with 016 to 019 (house rule 9).** l.5 says the woods were "*finally* floored with flowers" only "at the very end of May". But in 016 the thaw comes early in March and the bank is in bloom by mid-March, and in 017 the Hollow "turned green almost overnight" at the end of April. The late Gathering needs a reason other than late flowers.

### "Sherlock", facts and natural history

**Verdict: PASS WITH FIXES.**

| Check | Finding |
|---|---|
| Thermals over sunny ridges | **The physics is correct** (l.64): sun-heated slopes produce rising columns, and eagles soar them without flapping. The east face of a western ridge does take the morning sun first. **But the timing is wrong.** Usable thermals form from mid-morning, once the ground has warmed, and soaring raptors (bald eagles included) typically wait for them. Maverick sets off "just after sunrise" (l.52) and finds an unusually good thermal soon after (l.62). **Fix:** have him set off "once the sun was well up", or add a clause at l.62 ("by the time the sun had warmed the eastern face of the ridge"). The Wednesday thermal, passed after noon (l.347), is correct. |
| Valley fog lifting by late morning | **Correct.** This is radiation fog pooled in a valley after a clear, calm night, clear above (l.307 is accurate), burning off "a little before noon" (l.341). The wisps drifting up the valley sides are accurate. Maverick's reason for waiting (he cannot be sure of finding the ridge) is sound. |
| Eagle behaviour | Diurnal, no flying after dark: **correct** (l.213, l.464), and consistent with 010. Young eagles playing and chasing together in a thermal: **plausible**. Bald eagles do not really "loop the loop". They roll, tumble and dive, and paired eagles cartwheel. **Optional:** "loop" becomes "roll" (l.70, l.252–254, l.405). "Beavers do not climb trees" (l.195) is **correct**. Pink feathers (l.419) is flagged in the text itself as impossible, which is fine as comedy. Flight time over a ridge and back "before lunch" is **plausible**. |
| Apple-sapling planting in late May | **Acceptable with a tweak.** Bare-root apples are planted while dormant (late winter to early spring). By late May, saplings are in leaf. Moving them with roots "wrapped in sacking" (l.432, root-balled) is the right method and survivable if they are watered in. **Fixes:** l.466, "with their first leaves out", is wrong for late May (they would be in full young leaf), so change it to "with their new leaves". Ideally, add a clause that the youngsters water them in (l.438). A late planting season fits the coordinator's late-spring rationale (see M-2). |
| Fiddle strings | A "silver" string is accurate: older gut G strings were silver-wound. A plucked string ringing out is fine. **Error:** spare strings are not kept "unwound from the peg-box" (l.401). The peg-box holds the strings already on the instrument, wound on their pegs. Spares are kept coiled in the fiddle case. **Fix:** "took a spare string, coiled, from the fiddle case". |
| Rockslide after spring rains | **Correct.** Saturated slopes slip. |
| Sun and ridge geography | The sun setting behind Weather Ridge (west) at l.462 is **correct**. "The evening light was turning gold on Weather Ridge" (l.425) is slightly off, because the Hollow-facing (east) side of a western ridge is in shadow at sunset. **Optional:** "turning gold *over* Weather Ridge", as at l.213. |
| Late-May dusk | Back "before tea", then porch, tree and apology "nearly dark" (l.144–223): **plausible**, given late sunsets in late May. |

## Lock Three

### "Dr Noura", child development and emotional safety

**Verdict: PASS WITH FIXES.**

**What works:**
- **The emotional modelling is excellent.**
  - Maverick names the real reason for his fib: "I didn't want them to know I'd forgotten" (l.203). That is exactly the shame children feel, said in their words.
  - Benny's response is patient presence, not a lecture ("Go away." "No.", l.197–199).
  - The apology is specific and owned, with no excuses (l.225).
  - Both Buck (l.227–229) and the badger (l.386–390) meet it with acceptance, not punishment.
  - The message is that you can own a mistake and repair it, and that asking for help or using notes is strength, not weakness. It is healthy, and especially good for swaggering or perfectionist children.
- **Delaying gratification and self-regulation** (the fog, l.305–341; not looking at the thermal, l.347; "Can't stop! Message!", l.405) are shown at a level a five- to seven-year-old can copy.
- **Danger is handled as worry, not fear.** The rockslide is never shown happening and nobody is ever on the path. "Nobody on the high path" (l.454) closes the loop.

**Required fixes:**
- **N-1. Predator-and-prey connotation (Bible §15; l.37).** "The way a very large eagle looks at a small beaver", even with "great kindness", invites the image a raptor-and-rodent pairing carries, in a canon that forbids any predator-and-prey reference. Rewrite it, for example: "Maverick looked at him with great kindness, as a very tall friend looks at a very short one." The l.442 hare approaching "very shyly" is low risk and can stay, because the shyness is about Maverick's fame.
- **N-2. Safety: a youngster and a lit lamp (l.292).** Make the flapping blow the card and papers across the table, or knock Barney's tea cosy off, instead of making a flame flicker that a child steadies. If a lamp is mentioned, a grown-up lit it and it stands out of reach.
- **N-3. Safety: hot kettles (l.440).** Grown-ups make and pour the tea, and Reba hands round the cups and "tastes every cup to make sure it was right". This keeps the joke and the food-loving Reba.
- **N-4. Safety: Rusty and Beau's trek (l.325).** As written, two youngsters propose a walk of more than a day, with a night out, and no grown-up is involved. Either have Rusty offer "My dad could walk it, with Beau and me", or keep the offer without the timing. Maverick's refusal stays the point.
- **N-5. Shame load (l.193–229, l.321, l.362, l.458).** Trim Ch5's drooping by about a third (for example, compress l.201–207) and cut the Ch10 recap of apologies. A bedtime listener needs to see the shame resolved quickly.
- **N-6 (minor). l.313,** "a whole valley full of folk walking up the high path … towards a rockslide", is the only vivid peril image. It is acceptable, but keep it to one sentence and do not add to it.

### Narration readiness

**Ready after fixes.**
- **The card texts in capitals (l.240–244, l.272)** need a narrator note: read them as the card being spoken, not shouted. "LOW PASS!" (l.292) is a fine mid-story shout. Nothing loud happens after Ch8.
- **The mantra lines** (l.31, l.56, l.345, l.415) are excellent read-aloud rhythm, and children will chant them.
- **Long breath sentences** at l.5, l.60, l.70 and l.438 need breaking, or at least narrator pacing. l.60 (the flight-path list) and l.438 (the feast) are the heaviest.
