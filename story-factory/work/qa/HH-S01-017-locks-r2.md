### Weaknesses

1. **The rule 1 residual (l.313).** "They could see the whole of the day … like a map" is the narrator stating the theme just before the summit. It was required in round 1 and has not been removed. The panorama at l.311 does the work by itself.
2. **The last beat is a near-banned one (l.440).** "Nobody could hear anybody else say a single word. So nobody tried." is the banned "Nobody said anything" beat, given a reason. Together with Buck's wordless "He did not say anything" at l.428 and l.317, the silence motif is overused. Make the noise funny instead of silent. For example, Beau tries to say goodnight to the peepers and cannot even hear himself, so he peeps back at them instead.
3. **The inscription climax is a season pattern (advisory).** 014 (the slate), 015 (painted names), 016 ("Next year: bring Sadie Belle.") and 017 (HERE) all peak on a written word. 017's HERE is the best of them and is not "left for others for next time", so it stands. **018–022 must not use a written inscription as the emotional peak.**
4. **A small logic gap at Lookout Rock (l.305).**
   - Rusty goes there "all the time … by the top path", and the Bible says Buck goes there "now and then, to remember".
   - The X is a short climb above it, between the knobs, so either of them might have stumbled on the stump.
   - One clause fixes it, for example: "the top path comes along the ridge from the far side and never goes near the knobs."
5. **Beau's role is a little split.** He is the impatient one in Ch4 (the laurel), yet the patient looker in Ch3, Ch5, Ch6 and Ch9. "Bobcats are made for squeezing" (l.184) frames it as daring rather than haste, which is canon-true (athletic, daring). It is acceptable.
6. **Minor points:**
   - "Beau put his paws over his mouth so as not to laugh out loud" (l.432) has no motive. Why not laugh?
   - Rusty has a "pocket" again (l.440). See M-4.
