## Lock One — "Piers Morgan", senior editor and literary critic

### Score: 8.4 / 10 (pass mark 8.5): NOT YET PASSED, very close

This is a substantially better book. The mystery now has real investigative shape: hypothesis, test, failure, experiment, deduction, observation. Both principals are wrong once and right once, and the community scenes are warm and funny. The rabbits singing to an empty snag, answered by a flicker, is the best moment in the manuscript. The Ch10 fence-post landing ties Maverick's comic flaw to his growth with real elegance.

It falls just short of the pass mark for one reason: the revision introduced a logic contradiction at the heart of the mystery, plus a small evidence overreach in a story whose whole moral is evidence. Both are one-sentence fixes. Fix them and this clears 8.5.

### Strengths

- **The evidence method is dramatised, not described.** Rusty sweeps the trail at dusk "so I'll know one more true thing", and three new feathers appear on bare earth. This is the brief's moral in action, and something a child could do in a garden.
- **Rusty is now fallible.** "No treasure… I was wrong. He did not enjoy saying it. But it was true." This removes round 1's sensible-one-versus-brash-one imbalance.
- **Maverick's redemption is earned through competence.** His eagle eyes find the bird. Then "watching the post instead of watching yourself" pays off his very first scene. The landing in Ch10 ("He wobbled. He flapped. He stayed on.") is a lovely, child-sized triumph.
- **The community scene (Ch8) is now excellent.** Barney's kindness disguised as a joke, Brody's worried "we can't have the cake?", Reba's instant "That's the rule… It is now", and Sadie Belle's quiet "It was brave". Each voice is distinct and canon-true.
- **The rabbits' song (Ch8 setup, Ch9 payoff)** replaces the visual banner gag with something audible, tender and original.
- **The voices stay sharp**: "Thistle landing. Very advanced."; "Sitting on the treasure."; "That's quite clever. Did you just make that up?" / "Mostly."
- **Ch10 escapes the season's template.** It is a genuinely different close: dusk walk, bridge goodnight, Maverick watching the lights go out.

### Weaknesses

1. **Flicker logic contradiction (Ch6, para 278 against Ch6 para 252, Ch9 and Ch10).** The narrator says the birds are "shy travellers that passed through the Hollow in autumn on their way south, never stopped long, and flew off… the moment anybody came near". But the same chapter establishes "They live in it" (the snag). The birds land at noon in front of the entire Hollow, and one roosts there that evening. They cannot be both never-stopping travellers who flee at once *and* residents who perform for a crowd. The line also contradicts Rusty's own logic: he was confident they return to the snag every day. The para is a narrator-tell patch, and it reads as one.
2. **Evidence overreach in the evidence story (Ch4, para 170).** "I know there are new ones every morning, because I swept the trail and three more came." He has observed *one* morning. Buck then endorses this as a "good, true thing". In a story whose moral is "seek evidence before reaching conclusions", the hero over-generalising from one data point, approved by the wisdom figure, is exactly the error a sharp adult reader will catch. Fix: "there were new ones this morning".
3. **The fair-play clue was lost.** Buck's round-1 description of a real golden eagle (dark brown, gold only on the nape) was the one piece of evidence that tested *Maverick's* theory, as opposed to Rusty's. It has been removed together with the size error. The golden-eagle theory now survives untested until the flickers appear. *Recommended, not required:* restore a corrected version, either in Ch4 or as Rusty's later realisation.
4. **Ch3's rumour comedy has been flattened into summary (Ch4, para 154).** The round-1 version had live dialogue: Charlie's "Maybe it's made of sunshine!", Reba's "It might have been me." Now it is reported speech, and Charlie has no spoken line until Ch8. *Recommended:* restore one or two lines of live dialogue.
5. **A second aphorism sits next to #73 (Ch10).** "You were watching the post instead of watching yourself." It is charming, it is undercut nicely by "Mostly", and it is about a different lesson (self-consciousness). It is acceptable. Keep it only if the author is sure it does not dilute Buck's saying; I would keep it.
6. **Buck's "I've been wondering all day what I'd say to the pair of you"** slightly frames the wisdom as a prepared lesson, which the Bible discourages ("lands like a goodnight kiss, not a lesson"). *Optional:* let it fall more casually.
7. **Minor:**
   - "Barney, the younger beaver brother" (Ch8) is an expository tag in the middle of a scene. *Optional:* cut it.
   - Charlie calls him "Grandpappy Buck" (Ch9). Elsewhere, and in the Bible, he is always "Great-Grandpappy Buck". Use the full form, or confirm the short form with the founder.

### Required revisions (Lock One)

- **R1.** Resolve the flicker contradiction. Rewrite Ch6, para 278 so it agrees with the birds living in the snag. For example: "They could not ask the birds. Flickers, it turned out, were not chatty folk; they said 'kee-yer' and 'wick-a-wick', and that was about all, and they kept to their own busy business." Alternatively, the birds are shy and keep their distance but come and go from their snag. Delete "travellers… on their way south… never stopped long".
- **R2.** Ch4, para 170: change "there are new ones every morning" to "there were new ones this morning". Buck's "five good, true things" then stands.
