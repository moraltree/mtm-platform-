## Lock One — "Piers Morgan", senior editor (fresh read)

### Score: 8.6 / 10: PASSED (narrowly)

The revision does what a good editorial pass should: it puts the heroine's turning point on the page and gives her a reason to be admired at her own party. It also turns the low point into a scene where both characters behave recognisably and kindly. The story now earns its moral rather than announcing it. Two things keep it from scoring higher:
- a long post-wisdom coda that dilutes the lullaby close;
- an ensemble that remains a parade rather than a cast. Rusty and Sadie Belle in particular are close to walk-ons.

### Strengths

- **The teacup tower (Ch5)** is now the book's emotional engine. Charlie's thought "If I carried all the cups at once… She could sit down" (l.213) makes her mistake an act of love. The child listener sides with her, and the adult reader understands Maggie Mae's snap. That is exactly the double sympathy a story like this needs.
- **Ch7, "On Charlie's Doorstep"**, is the best chapter.
  - "It felt like I was too little and too messy to be your friend. Like I wasn't in the picture" (l.303) quietly echoes Maggie Mae's "picture she had held in her head" (l.256). It also nods to Pip's canonical dread of being "too little".
  - The mutual apology rings true.
  - The laugh at the boot-scraper is a true release.
- **The "Right on time" refrain (Ch8)** turns the comic stagger into a demonstration of grace. Children will join in with it. The personalised touches (the gatepost cushion, the named honey jar, the quilt for Buck) show Southern hospitality rather than describing it.
- **The comic set-pieces are retained:** the cake lifting the oven door, "free o'clock", the ladybird crossing out MAGNIFICENT, Maverick's "fashionable eagle", Barney who "cannot whisper", and Rusty's shortcut home and back again (l.426).
- **The coda's best lines** are "Same again next year?" / "Same again next year. Assistant." (l.438–440). It is a perfect, earned callback. Washing up "one cup at a time, which took a great deal longer… and neither of them minded" (l.434) re-stages the moral without a word of telling.
- **Ch6 is now shown, not told:** "found that she did not want to look at it" (l.262).

### Weaknesses

1. **The close is long and the wisdom is buried (Ch10).** After Buck's #85 at l.420 there are roughly 20 more paragraphs: departures, gifts, Buck's escort, washing up, the walk to Charlie's, the walk home, the kitchen, the list. Each beat is warm, and the "everyone safely home" sequence is soothing in itself. But the house structure wants the wisdom to land close to the goodnight, and the energy rises again with Barney's shuffle and Rusty's shortcut gag. The final image is Maggie Mae awake in her kitchen, not anyone settling to sleep. This is not a failure, because the founder allows varied staging, but it is the main thing between this draft and a 9.
2. **The final image is unclear in audio (l.448).** "She took it down, folded it in half, and tucked Charlie's card in front of it, where she would see it every morning." A listener cannot picture where the folded list and the card end up. A clearer version: "…and pinned Charlie's card up in its place, where she would see it every morning."
3. **The ensemble is thin.** Sadie Belle has one clause (a pot of chrysanthemums) plus the Beau blush. Rusty has two shortcut gags. Brody's banjo apprenticeship and Ch2 reading scene have been cut. Benny's best line from round 1 ("It means you can choose the biggest") has been cut; measuring a ladybird is a weaker replacement. Nobody's particular gift changes anything. For a two-hander brief this is acceptable, but restoring Benny's verdict costs one line and is worth it.
4. **Sadie Belle is out of order (l.358).** She arrives "at three", but she is narrated after Reba "at four", which jolts the clockwise listing the chapter has trained the listener to follow. Move her up beside Barney and Benny.
5. **Maggie Mae's voice is still lightly Southern.** With "sugar" cut to 3, her distinct Southern belle idiom rests on a single "Oh, my stars". Her hospitality is now shown in action, but one or two more characteristic turns ("Well, I declare", "come sit a spell") would make her *sound* as sophisticated as she behaves.
6. **Small craft slips:**
   - "It was a kind thought. It was the kindest thought Charlie had all day." (l.215) implies her other thoughts were unkind. Suggest "It was a kind thought, and a very Charlie one."
   - "the guests arriving all at once and in order" (l.335) is self-contradictory. Use "all together, at three o'clock exactly".
   - Remaining stock phrasing: "The words were out before she could catch them" (l.237), "Her blue eyes were shining" (l.315) and "neither of them said anything for quite a long time" (l.317). These are tolerable but generic.
   - "Magnificent" ×10 and "perfect" ×9 are thematic motifs and acceptable.
7. **The Wisdom/emblem tension remains (minor; founder's choice is logged).** "Nobody remembers what you owned" is spoken minutes after a treasured *owned* cup has become the story's emblem. The cup is valued for its story, not its ownership, so the line can stand. A sharper adult reader may still notice it.

### Required revisions for Lock One

None. Lock One passes at 8.6. Weaknesses 1, 2 and 4 are **strongly recommended** polish: shorten or soften the post-wisdom coda, end on a sleepier line, clarify the final image, and reorder Sadie Belle. Weaknesses 3, 5 and 6 are optional.

## Lock Three — "Dr Noura" (safeguarding, emotional safety, bedtime) and narration (fresh read)

*This is an AI role-based assessment, not clinical advice.*

### Result: PASS WITH NOTES

### Safeguarding findings

1. **The sharp words and sending Charlie home: handled well, and improved.**
   - The outburst is stressed, not cruel ("Please… Please just go home"), and follows a visibly overwhelmed Maggie Mae: flour on her ear, talking to herself. Children see *why* it happened.
   - Charlie goes where she was told, her own home, so she is safe throughout.
   - The repair is exemplary:
     - Maggie Mae comes to her;
     - she acknowledges Charlie's kind intention ("I didn't see it then. I only saw the cup");
     - Charlie names the hurt ("too little and too messy to be your friend");
     - both own their part, followed by a hug, re-invitation and laughter.
   - This now models emotional literacy and mutual accountability very well for ages 4–8.
2. **Imitation risks.**
   - *Climbing on a chair and carrying a tall stack of crockery (Ch5):* this is the most imitable act in the story. It is mitigated: the consequence is shown, and Charlie herself says "I should have asked first. Especially the tower." No change required.
   - *Drying up while standing on a chair* (l.434): an older figure is present; low risk.
   - *Oven:* Maggie Mae handles it with a cloth. *Broken cup:* no shards, the handle only, and the mending is done by Maggie Mae. *Cordial* is warm, not hot. All low risk.
   - *Lanterns:* Buck (the adult) lights and carries them: compliant.
   - **However (new):** l.442 has Maggie Mae walk Charlie home through the dark while "the glass lanterns she had left glowing in her rose arch" burn unattended. It is a lovely image, but it shows lit flames left unattended outdoors among roses and leaf piles. That cuts against the founder's safeguarding default (adults handle flames). It is a cheap fix: Buck leaves Maggie Mae a lantern to carry and puts out the rest himself, or the path is lit by starlight or moonlight and "the lamp in her own window".
3. **Getting home safely after dark (Ch10).**
   - Buck escorts Reba and Beau, plus the brothers (contradicted at l.426; see L2).
   - Brody, Sadie Belle and Rusty appear to walk home alone after dark, and Rusty takes a shortcut.
   - Maggie Mae walks Charlie home, where her mother receives her (excellent), then walks back alone.
   - The overall effect is reassuring: everyone gets home, and an adult is visibly involved. It would be tidier, and it would fix the L2 continuity error, if Buck's lantern escort covered "everyone whose path lay his way, and the rest went home together in a little lantern-lit troop". Recommended, and it can be combined with the L2 fix.
4. **Trust in responsible adults: good.** Buck does not solve the conflict but sees everyone home. Charlie's mother receives her at the door.
5. **The moral is shown, not preached: good.** It is now shown chiefly through the tower, the doorstep scene, the refrain, the cup and the one-cup-at-a-time washing up.
6. **Frightening content:** none.
7. **Bedtime wind-down: adequate, not ideal.**
   - Ch9 and Ch10 slow nicely: lanterns, the moth, a slow tune, stars.
   - After the wisdom, the energy briefly rises (Barney's shuffle down the lane, Rusty's shortcut back and forth) before settling again.
   - The last image is Maggie Mae in a warm kitchen with a glowing fire, which is calm, but no one is shown going to sleep.
   - Recommend one closing line of sleep before "Goodnight, Hickory Hollow". For example: Maggie Mae going up to bed, or the Hollow's lamps going out. Round 1 also flagged that S01-001's lamps-going-out image should not be copied verbatim.

### Narration findings

1. **No visual-only jokes remain.** The card is spelled correctly and read aloud cleanly. "Free o'clock" works perfectly in audio.
2. **The "Right on time" refrain** is excellent for read-aloud participation.
3. **Attribution is clear throughout.** In the two-voice exchanges in Ch7 and Ch10, the unattributed lines alternate unambiguously ("Free o'clock?" l.329; "Same again next year?" l.438).
