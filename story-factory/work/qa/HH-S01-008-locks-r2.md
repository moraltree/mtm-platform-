## Lock One

### "Piers Morgan", senior editor and literary critic

### Score: 8.4 / 10 — NOT YET PASSED (pass mark 8.5; very close)

This is now a good book. The revisions did more than tick boxes: they made it better.
- **Barney's tower.** His offering it first, "before anybody else could speak", turns him from someone forgiven into someone who repairs. His offer is also what tips Reba from hesitation into giving. That is cause and effect between characters, which is the ensemble at its best.
- **The new climax.** Buck reading the gaps along the line is the right climax for a gratitude story. The audience sees the sacrifice through the eyes of the person it was for.
- **Charlie's "And that was all."** Her plain thanks is far stronger than the greeting-card line it replaced.
- **Maverick's laugh line** is placed exactly where the evening needed air.

Three things keep it just short of 8.5. Each is small, but two of them sit on the book's most important lines.

### Strengths (new or confirmed)
- **Barney's arc is complete.** He breaks the lantern, Benny sits with him without words, he gives his best bit first, he asks for "just enough", he gives his thanks, and he presents the lantern. The arc is honest and it does not shame him.
- **Benny's pencil and his frame-ring.** He gives the ring from his own frame (l.281, l.308) and he pockets his pencil. The careful planner gives up both perfection and his own work, and the text never says so.
- **The climax image (l.381).** The gaps are listed in the order Buck would see them, ending on the pink bow. It is controlled and well judged.
- **A cleaner close.** The quilt and the Wisdom now arrive without commentary. #15 lands as a gift.
- **Strong comic and character texture throughout** (confirmed from round 1).

### Weaknesses
1. **The thematic image miscounts (l.334).** "Nine lanterns with holes in, and one with every hole filled": there are ten young friends and all ten gave. A parent or child counting the ten painted friends will notice. This is the book's key line. (Beau's gift is two sticks "from the back", so his lantern arguably has no visible hole. But the line claims "everybody's had a gap", so either way the number must be made true or removed.)
2. **The moral is still doubled (l.334 and l.336).** Narrator and child say the same thing in consecutive paragraphs. Charlie's line is the better one, because it is a discovery. With Barney's thanks and "It's better" also in place, l.334 is surplus.
3. **The close contradicts itself on the banjo (l.428 and l.438).** Buck hums, and then the last paragraph gives "the soft notes of the banjo". A narrator will stumble on it and a listener will be confused. It also lets the Bible's signature banjo (§10, Canon 3, and the founder default that Buck is banjo master) slip out of the close.
4. **Minor:**
   - Benny "knelt down" (l.230) when he is already sitting shoulder to shoulder with Barney.
   - Maggie Mae's yellow strip (l.279) and Brody's corner (l.277) are promised but never shown being used in Ch8. One clause would do it.
   - "Out of the wind" (l.349) now reads oddly two lines after the wind "had dropped to nothing".
   - Charlie looks at the lantern "glowing by Buck's door" (l.432), but it now hangs from "a hook on the porch rail" (l.412).

### Required revisions (Lock One)
1. **R2-1.** Make l.334's count true. Use "Ten lanterns with holes in, and one with every hole filled", or drop the number. If Beau's stays hole-free, adjust "Everybody's had a gap".
2. **R2-2.** Keep Charlie's "The holes are where we put the pieces" (l.336). Cut or shrink the narrator's echo at l.334 so the idea is stated once. (This can be combined with R2-1, for example by keeping only "Everybody's had a gap. A missing edge, a missing corner, a missing piece of sky.")
3. **R2-3.** Resolve the humming/banjo contradiction. Preferably, Buck takes up his banjo after the Wisdom: this restores the signature close, and his line from Ch2 ("better at banjo than paper") pays off. Otherwise, change l.438 to his humming.
4. **R2-5.** Small slips: Benny "knelt" becomes "leaned over" (l.230); add a clause in Ch8 for Maggie Mae's yellow strip and Brody's corner; cut "out of the wind" (l.349) or reword it to "under the barn roof"; change "by Buck's door" (l.432) to "on the porch rail".
