   - It is close to word for word 003's turning-point line ("the most important part of a party is the folk who come", 003 l.307). 003 is the story the slate flag tells this one to differ from.
   - Maggie Mae's "It's the most memorable one" (l.256) then states the premise aloud. Sadie Belle's "Because he was glad we came" (l.297) states the friendship moral a second time.
   - "So that, in the end, it was a proper picnic after all" (l.371) is a mild narrator summary.
   - Taken together, the friendship and memorable-not-perfect message is told three times before Buck speaks.
   - l.256 can stay, because it is the hinge of the turn and is a feeling, not a maxim. l.84 needs reshaping, and l.297 should follow from something shown.
2. **The l.84 plant is never paid off by Beau.** "Somewhere at the back of his head, the thing she had said stayed, and waited" (l.88) promises that Beau will remember or act on it. He never does.    - l.25: "the most magnificent picnic Hickory Hollow has ever seen".
   - Beau's flamboyance is canon. But l.68 makes the surprise a display of his success rather than a gift. Recast it in terms of gratitude.
6. **Chronology and structure.**

7. **Small logic slips.**

   - **The temperature.** The pie is a "warm purple pudding" (l.209), but it was baked on Friday evening.

## LOCK THREE: "Dr Noura" (child safety, wellbeing, bedtime suitability) and narration readiness

### Dr Noura: verdict PASS WITH CONDITIONS

**Conditions (required)**
- **N-1. Sheltering in a tree during rain (l.189).**
  - The text justifies it in a single narrated rule: "there was no thunder in the shower, Maverick said, not a rumble, just rain, so it was safe to shelter under it."
  - That is factually true, and better than ignoring 024. But to a young listener the takeaway can easily become "trees are fine if *Maverick* says so". 024's clear message was "never shelter under a tree in a thunderstorm". Mixed messages across adjacent stories are a real modelling risk.
  - Preferred fix (with M-1a): shelter in the Mill's cart shed or under its eaves, a building. That removes the issue entirely.
  - If a hollow tree is kept (M-1b), drop "so it was safe", and give a child the rule in action: "If we hear one rumble, we run for the Barn." Have Maverick go to the Barn rafters as in 024 (M-2), so the safest choice is visibly modelled by somebody.
- **N-2. Rusty following the cloth down the creek alone (l.271, l.363).**
  - A child following a floating object along a creek bank is a classic drowning scenario. The story otherwise models it beautifully: Maggie Mae says "We'll ask the grown-ups to look along the bank" (l.125), and nobody goes after the cloth.
  - Make it explicit that Rusty saw where it caught "from the path" and **did not try to reach it**, and went straight for his father. A clause at l.271 does it, for example: "had seen where it caught, and come away from the water without touching it, just as everybody knew to".
  - Rusty's father's retrieval (l.363) is correct adult handling of water.

**Advisory**
- **Maggie Mae "drying her bow by the stove" (l.392).** This is Beau's imagining ("probably"). Maggie Mae lives alone and has a fire at home in 003, so it is low risk. But under Rule 7 (flames) and the decision log's stance on young skunks and tools (023), "hanging her bow on the kitchen line" or "by the window" removes the question at no cost.
- **Charlie running to the Mill alone in daytime (l.168).** Acceptable. It is broad daylight, the Mill is "not far", and the errand is to fetch an adult for a hazard, which is exactly what children should do. Charlie fetching Sadie Belle (l.271) is likewise fine.
- **Unsupervised outing.** Three youngsters picnic on a creek-side bank with no adult present. They stay on the bank and never go near the water, and an adult is fetched at once for each hazard. This is consistent with S01's daytime freedoms, so acceptable. If the coordinator wants extra cover, Beau's mother can know the plan ("back by supper") in Ch2.
- **Raw flour paste.** Reba "only tasted it twice" (l.318). This is comic and harmless in a story, but raw flour is not a food. Keep it if desired, as Reba's canon food curiosity.

**Wellbeing and tone**
- **The wasps are handled well.** The danger is named quietly, the instruction is clear ("Slowly. Don't flap. Leave the basket"), nobody is stung, the adult's retrieval is calm, and "They were here first, I expect" (l.170) adds respect for nature. This is model behaviour.
- **The laughter turn is well judged.** Beau's distress is brief and held kindly. Maggie Mae apologises at once and makes clear they are laughing at the cloth and the ants, not at him (l.232–238). Beau then chooses to join in. This keeps clear of the Bible's prohibition on humour at a character's expense.
- **Sadie Belle's crush** is gentle and innocent, and Maggie Mae notices it silently (l.299). That is appropriate.
- **No predator/prey content, menace, sarcasm or dated references.** The ants eat cheese sandwiches. The wasps are simply going about their business.
- **The close is calm and drowsy.** The cooling dusk, the slowing drips, chin on paws and a quiet laugh. There is no jolt.

### Narration readiness: GOOD, minor items

Coming over Weather Ridge…" are easy to voice, and the impression joke (l.242–246) needs Beau to imitate Maverick's voice audibly. Mark it as a voice cue.
- **l.373 (pluperfect flashback inside Ch9) is confusing when heard.** A listener cannot tell whether Buck has already sat down. Reorder it (L1-6).
- **l.74 ("On Thursday afternoon") after Friday night** will make a listener feel the story has jumped back. Reorder it (L1-6).
- **Long sentences to split for breath:**
  - l.105, a 130-plus-word paragraph with Rusty's cameo in one run;
  - l.371, one 70-word sentence;
  - l.60, the cooking list ending in the pie;
  - l.189, where the safety clause interrupts the dash for shelter.
