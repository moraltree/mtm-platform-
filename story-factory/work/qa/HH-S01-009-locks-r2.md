## Lock One

### "Piers Morgan", senior editor and literary critic

### Score: 8.6 / 10 — PASSED (pass mark 8.5)

The revision does what was asked without flattening the story. The best change is restraint:
- Benny turns his mug and says nothing else (l.236).
- Barney watches the miller's wife "and did not say anything at all" (l.391).
- Buck's wisdom is the only telling.

The moral now lands through three shown acts: the snowflake delivery, the seventh square, and the heap reborn as a sledging slope. Ch7 now has life. Maggie Mae's "Sugar" is the first time this season her Southern-belle register has felt natural. The new close (three lines of pawprints, Buck's slow one in the middle) is the best ending image of the batch, and it is entirely fresh against house rule 5.

### Strengths (new or confirmed)

- **Opening and Ch2–3 comedy** are unchanged and still excellent.
- **The turning point** (Ch6) is cleaner without Benny's argument. The question does the work.
- **The ensemble** is fully used, and the jobs are now partly dramatised.
- **Mountain Wisdom #12** is exact, has no lead-in, and is new to the season. Its fit with the gentleness of the operation is good.
- **The close** is fresh in location (mill step, creek path), image (pawprints) and companionship (Buck walking them home).

### Remaining weaknesses (not blocking)

1. **The arc still echoes S01-003** (a grand plan fails, the small gesture wins). The brief mandates this. The execution is now distinct enough (an audience of one, the snow science), so no action is needed.
2. **The "I meant it" gag is used twice.** Maverick's "Meant to do that" (l.68) and Beau's "that had been part of the plan" (l.311) are the same joke. Optional: give Beau a different topper.
3. **Ch5–6 remain the slowest stretch.** Acceptable for a bedtime story.
4. **Geography of the heaps** (see L2-1) is a continuity matter, not a craft one.
5. **Small overstatements:** "the best thing that had ever happened to her" (l.403); "biggest… idea he had ever had" (l.31). Optional.

## Lock Two

### "Moneypenny" (canon and continuity) and "Sherlock" (facts)

### Result: PASS WITH NOTES (four required line fixes)

**Cast:** all eleven are correct as in round 1. Maggie Mae's "Sugar" fits v1.1's Southern belle. No retired names appear. Background adults (the miller, the miller's wife, the rabbit family and the littlest rabbit) are consistent with earlier stories.

**Places:** the Great Barn in Fern Meadow is canon (v1.0 l.536). "Behind the Great Barn, at the far end of Fern Meadow" is a new but harmless micro-location.

**Timeline:**
- **Day 1:** powder, then the afternoon build behind the barn; grown-ups clearing paths; warm wind at night.
- **Day 2:** thaw; the mill; Buck's forecast "that evening"; the velvet, the teacup trial and the stone path.
- **Day 3:** frost back by lunchtime; snow in the afternoon; the window; dusk on the step; sledging; nearly dark; the walk home.

Coherent and stated. Small point: "the cold came back" in the afternoon (l.334), but the frost had already "come back hard" by lunchtime (l.336). Readable as an overnight frost, so this is optional.

### Required continuity and fact fixes

- **L2-1 — The hidden palace is visible from the mill.** The revision hides the palace "round behind the Great Barn… where nobody ever goes" so that no grown-up sees it. Yet from the mill step:
  - Barney looks at the heaps (l.393–395);
  - Buck "looked out at the meadow, where the smooth white heaps of the palace lay… with the little sledging tracks" (l.426).

  The miller's wife also watches the meadow from her window all day (l.195). Separately, on Day 3, everybody goes into "the open middle of Fern Meadow… past the grey heaps where the palace had been" (l.342), but the heaps are at the far end behind the barn.

  Fix:
  - l.393–395: Barney *thinks* of the heaps, or looks "towards the Great Barn".
  - l.426: Buck sees the sledgers coming back, or the tracks, rather than the heaps. Alternatively, cut the heaps from his gaze.
  - l.342: make it "past the Great Barn" or drop the clause.
- **L2-2 — "Three winters" survives at l.217.** Benny says "She hasn't seen the first snow up close for three winters." This now contradicts both l.205 ("First snow I've missed since I was a girl") and S01-007. Change it to, for example, "It's the first snow she's ever missed."

  Related, optional: l.383 has the narrator say she is "out in the first snow of the winter after all", but this is the second snowfall. Barney's "We brought you the first snow" (l.356) is fine as a child's claim. For the narrator, prefer "out in the snow after all".
- **L2-3 — "a black mitten" (l.426).** Since C1/C6 she has the seventh velvet square, not a mitten. Change it to "the little square of black velvet" or similar.
- **L2-4 — Naked-eye "stars, each one with six arms" (l.346).** One of these five flakes is the plain hexagon that l.362 describes. Change it to "tiny white stars and specks, every one six-sided" or "tiny six-sided sparks". l.372, "every board brought new stars", is acceptable as loose description, but "new shapes" would be safer.
