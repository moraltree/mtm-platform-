### "Sherlock", facts and logic

**Verdict: PASS WITH ONE REQUIRED FIX (R2-1).**
- **Blackberry season** (mid-July, southern Appalachians): correct (late June to August by elevation).
- **Wasps** (l.13, l.117–121): correct. Wasps work ripe fruit from midsummer. Stillness, an open exit and no swatting or flapping is sound advice.
- **Bees** (l.13): now correct, on the last flowers. *Advisory:* the close lists the top-half berries as "left for the bees in the morning" (l.440). Bees rarely feed on ripe fruit, so drop "the bees" or replace them with another creature (A-2).
- **Stain:**
  - an immediate cold soak (l.248–250) is correct;
  - "Hot water can set berry juice in" is now appropriately hedged;
  - pressing from the back without rubbing (l.383–385) is correct;
  - lemon plus sun as a bleach on white cotton or linen is correct;
  - fading to "almost nothing" over a late summer afternoon and evening is plausible after a long cold soak;
  - "cold washing-up water" (l.248) is fine; soapy cold water helps;
  - **R2-1:** anthocyanin pigment reddens in acid, so the *pink* should appear after the lemon, not during the plain-water rinse. As written, l.385 has it turn pink before the lemon, and l.387 then attributes the pink to the lemon.
- **Gray catbird** (l.442): correct. It is a common Appalachian summer resident that roosts and nests in dense thickets and brambles, and eats berries heavily in summer. Mice (white-footed and deer mice) eat blackberries. Correct.
- **Timeline:** the second picking ends at 14:30. Then cleaning, assembling about an hour, baking about an hour, cooling and the stain work in "late afternoon" into evening sun, then supper. Mid-July sunset in the southern Appalachians is around 20:45. Consistent.
- **Arithmetic:** 4 + 8 = 12 pies; three baskets for eight pies; 843 berries. Consistent. Brody's high berries count into Reba's basket (l.311), and l.336 now says "it had", not "she picked". Fine.
