## Lock One

### "Piers Morgan", senior editor and literary critic

### Score: 8.6 / 10. PASSED (pass mark 8.5), conditional on fixes R2-1 to R2-4 below

This revision is a real improvement, not a cosmetic one.

**What improved**

- **The three unlocks now run on different engines.**
  - Benny's is a discovery in Reba's hands.
  - Brody's opens with a repair. "I'm sorry I laughed… At the dragon sitting on the tree." / "It was funny. I just didn't want it to be." (l.231–233) That reply is the best new line in the draft: honest, unhurt, and very Brody.
  - Sadie Belle's barrier is now purely the faces. "I could tell it to you. Just you. But not to everybody." (l.288) It also seeds Charlie's idea without handing it to her, and Charlie still supplies the method (sitting front, back to the room, l.304).
- **Chapter Seven now reads as a lively round, not a list** (l.325–337). "Spoiled his best bit" (l.329) and Reba's father (l.333) are quicker and funnier for being compressed.
- **The saying now stands alone.** l.432, "one by one, all the way to the end", is a look, not a paraphrase. #20 at l.434 lands with no lead-in, croaked at real cost. Rule 1 is now fully met.
- **The new close is fresh** and belongs to this story alone (l.452–458).
  - The miller nearly wipes the slate "as he wiped the flour off everything else", then stops and hangs it by the door "keeping its words safe until next winter".
  - It is a quiet, adult-scale grace note that gives Buck's joke a year's life.
  - It contains none of the banned beats and no tracks or glow. The scene winds down the way the Bible asks: embers, empty cushions, bed.
- **Benny's fever-week recital** (l.356) is better than the single number. Seven different counts read slowly make the love concrete, and Barney's wordless "mouth opened, and closed again" (l.358) is better than a narrated hush.

**Why the score is not higher**

- The emotional climax now contains a contradiction, caused by the S4 fix (R2-2 below).
- One of the r1 continuity fixes was missed in Ch8 (R2-1).
- Two hush and stillness phrasings remain around Brody's story (l.376).
- The structure is still three parallel arcs told in sequence on the night: Benny, then Brody, then Sadie Belle. This is sound and suits the crowding brief, but it is predictable. It is not a required change.

## Lock Two

### "Moneypenny", canon and continuity: PASS WITH NOTES (conditional on R2-1)

The r1 items M2–M6 are resolved, and so is most of M1. The cast is exactly the eleven, with no retired names. Charlie's father appears for the first time on page (l.292); this is new, and minor.

**Required fixes**

- **R2-1. l.348 still says "from my door to my brother's door".**
  - This contradicts l.184 in the same story, and canon: the brothers share a lodge (002 l.532, 009 l.5 and l.432).
  - Replace it with, for example: "That's how many steps it is from our front door to Grandpappy Buck's porch and back home again. I've counted them every morning for a year."
  - This must be fixed before sign-off.
- **R2-4. Gate and doorstep staging in Ch6.**
  - Charlie is found waiting "at the gate of Rose Arch Cottage… with her father beside her" (l.292), but then thinks "sitting quite still on the doorstep" (l.298).
  - Make one of them consistent, for example "sitting quite still on the gatepost step".
  - Also, Maggie Mae's "Wait here." (l.290) now leads nowhere, because she is walked home and does not return that night. Change it to "Leave it with me."

**Advisory**

- **A1. Barney's birthday entry (l.352).** "I ran home, because I'd made him a present and I couldn't wait to give it to him" sits awkwardly with "Barney and I go round every morning" (l.184), because Barney would be beside him. One clause fixes it: "and Barney raced me, because he knew there was a present".
- **A2. Charlie and the brothers on the Wednesday morning.** l.5 has Charlie alone on Buck's porch. Since the brothers now visit every morning, a reader might ask where they were. This is harmless and can be left as is.
- **A3 (also Lock One). The close's family resemblance.** The close is a kept written message displayed for others. That echoes 003 (Charlie's card tucked where Maggie Mae will see it every morning) and 011 (Reba's note tied on the Mending Branch). It is staged differently, it is the miller's choice and not a child's, and it is not a banned beat. Keep it, but do not use a displayed note or card to close any further S01 stories.
- **A4. l.376: "had never once in all those years asked what had become of it".** As noted in r1 (M8), this sits uneasily with Brody tending the tree behind the Mill for years in a small valley. "had never once said a word about it" agrees better with Buck's knowing pat at l.448.

### "Sherlock", facts and plausibility: PASS WITH NOTES

**Required fixes**

- **R2-2. The footprints no longer make sense.**
  - The new instruction is accurate deer behaviour: "walk behind me… put your hooves in my prints, where I've already made the way" (l.401). Deer do use one another's trails in deep snow.
  - But the text still says the prints were "A big set and a little set, side by side" (l.288 and l.401). And at the climax, "Yours are straight and mine go everywhere" (l.405).
  - If the fawn stepped in her grandmother's prints, there would be one line of prints, and not a wobbly one.
  - Simplest fix: keep the instruction for the way out, and add after "Fern Meadow and back" in l.401: "and on the way home, when she had the hang of it, her grandmother let her walk beside her, on her own". Then "side by side" and "mine go everywhere" are both true.
  - l.288 can stand as Sadie Belle's summary.
- **R2-3. l.376.** Rephrase "never once… asked" (A4) and "wrote nothing on his slate at all", the last remaining "did not say anything" variant. For example: "…and his eyes wet behind his spectacles, and his chalk quite still in his paw."
- **R2-5. Wording: "nearly twice as big" (l.178).** 896–920 is more than twice 412, which would be 824. Change it to "more than twice as big". Reba's "That's twice as many" (l.192) is fine as a child's estimate.

**Advisory**

- **A5. Lanterns (l.442 and l.458).** The miller has already put out the lanterns "one by one, until only the fire was left" (l.442). Later he "put out the last lantern" (l.458). Make the second "took up his own lantern", or "blew out his candle", before going up to bed.
- The new hickory scale (l.227) is plausible. The new step figures are plausible: a detour round Fern Meadow adds about 500 steps and varies with where the flowers were, and the 402 for running home is correct because running strides are longer. Long-pole icicle measuring (l.119) is plausible. Banking the fire for the night (l.452) is correct practice.
