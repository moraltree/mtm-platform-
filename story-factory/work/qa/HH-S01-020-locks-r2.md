## Lock One

### "Piers Morgan", senior editor and literary critic

### Score: 8.6 / 10. PASSED (pass mark 8.5), subject to the light line edits N2-1 and N2-2

The revision fixes everything structural. The canon contradiction is gone, and the replacement garble ("The Old Mill." "It's… big.") is funnier than the original. The moral now sits alone at l.421, with nothing before it that aphorises it and nothing after it that recaps it. The close is new to the series and earns its place:
- Maverick's single low flyover, with "every youngster in the meadow" waving from both valleys (l.446), is a lovely public redemption that costs him no swagger;
- the barn heap of mixed youngsters fulfils §6 (the festival ends with Buck's last story);
- the closing image of the badger's one long note, the planted row, and the washed kettles is quiet and drowsy, and it is not a kept token, a note left for others, or "next year".

### Strengths (new or improved)

- **The Old Mill garble** (l.103–105, l.172–174) is better comedy than the Great Barn version. The badger's "Well, that's new. But if Buck says the Mill…" is a gentle laugh that also sets up the trust Maverick is spending.
- **"Delivered. They were thrilled." said twice** (l.148, l.158) reads as a child's dodge, so Benny's "Can you read it back?" lands harder.
- **Ch9 without Buck's questions is stronger.** Buck waits, Maverick volunteers "And about the kettles" (l.413), and he owns the last embarrassment himself. That is better characterisation and better rule-2 compliance.
- **The emotional proportion is now right.** The shame is short, and the repair and comedy dominate.
- **The close widens the circle** (Bible §15 practical guide), with two valleys mixed in one heap. "Come over. Come and see us. We've missed you." (l.458) quietly states the reunion theme of the founder brief's "neighbours across the ridge" without touching the moral.

### Weaknesses (residual)

1. **Buck's barn story partly re-tells the lesson (house rule 1, moderate; N2-1).** At l.454 the messengers "read each other's messages out loud, slowly, to be sure". That is the read-back method from Ch6 and Ch8, restated after the wisdom, and it puts a *written* message at the emotional peak of the close (the "peak built on a written inscription" concern from recent reviewers). The story's real point is reconciliation ("We've missed you"), which is not the moral. **Fix:** "and *told* each other their messages" (cut "read … out loud, slowly, to be sure"). This also removes the second "to be sure".
2. **The leads are offstage for the final image.** Maverick leaves at l.446, which is correct under Buck's rule, and Benny's last line is at l.440. It is acceptable: Maverick's flyover is his close, and Benny's count is his. A half-clause placing Benny in the heap at l.448–450 would tie it off. Optional.
3. **Repetition tics** ("in years", "finest", "to be sure"; N2-4).
