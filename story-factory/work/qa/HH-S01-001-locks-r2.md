## Lock One — "Piers Morgan", senior editor and literary critic

### Score: 8.6 / 10 — PASS

This is now a publishable season opener once the line-level items below are done. The revision did the hard thing properly: it fixed the structure rather than papering over it. Buck's arc (recognition → a well-meant silence → a public apology to a small child) adds an emotional layer the first draft lacked. "I ought to have spoken up sooner… That was my mistake, little one" (l.333) and Charlie's "She had never heard a grown-up say sorry to her quite like that before" (l.335) are the best new lines. The score is not higher because of a residue of stock phrasing, a still-wrong count, and two small physical inconsistencies a sharp adult reader will notice.

### Strengths

- **Coherent mystery with fair clues, now pointed exactly at the brief.** Storm → leaves → "something went glub-hup, right underneath me" on the woodpile (l.105) → Benny's lines crossing "right under the end of Buck's porch" (l.192) → the basin under the porch. A child can solve it one beat before Charlie does.
- **The laughter chain (l.180–182)** is the best structural improvement: joy travelling friend to friend, rabbit to miller's wife to "somebody up on the meadow… without the faintest idea why". It dramatises the moral before anyone states it.
- **Every theory is in character**, and two pay off later (Benny's loose board, Brody's song). Barney's "fish with the hiccups" is funnier than the ghost was, and better for bedtime.
- **Ch5's adult intervention** is understated and exact. It raises the emotional stakes without frightening anyone.
- **Ch6 keeps Charlie's agency.** She has already decided before Maggie Mae arrives (l.278–280), and Maggie Mae's "May I come and sit?" asks rather than rescues.
- **The voice is cleaner throughout.** The Carolina wren's "tea-kettle, tea-kettle" (l.13) is a better and more accurate opening sound. The ending (Brody asleep "with the little banjo still in his arms"; the rain-pipe "laughing in its sleep") remains excellent.

### Weaknesses

1. **Residual stock phrasing:** "For one long, ridiculous, wonderful moment" (l.184); "Brody's face lit up like a lamp" (l.426). There are two "the way…" similes (l.27, l.64).
2. **The count is still wrong** (l.143: "Nine ideas"). Simplest fix: "Ten friends. More ideas than you can shake a stick at. One of them is a jealous jar of jam." Or "six ideas".
3. **Maggie Mae's "I couldn't, at their age"** (l.129) is confusing. She is a young skunk, so the line makes her sound like a grown-up, and "their" has no clear referent. Read aloud, it sounds like a slip. Suggest "I never can keep a surprise in."
4. **Buck's Ch1 resolution conflicts with Ch5.** "I'll tell them. When they ask. If they ask." (l.33): Charlie all but asks in Ch5 (l.199) and Buck deflects. Either change l.33 (e.g. "Let's see who finds it first") or let Ch5 show that she stated it rather than asked. Small, but it is exactly the kind of seam this story has already been caught on once.
5. **Two dangling gifts.** Maggie Mae's basket of late roses for Buck (l.44) and Reba's jam "as a present" (l.105) are never delivered. One clause each at the supper table (roses in a jug, the half jar of jam beside the cornbread) would give the adult reader a neat close and Reba a gentle comic beat.
6. **Over-long sentences** at l.372 (the crowd sentence) and l.411 (the Sadie Belle/Beau sentence).
7. **Ch5 search** is shorter and better (Benny's board-tapping gives it purpose) but is still a roll-call. Acceptable; not required.

### Required revisions (Lock One)

- R2-2: fix the count (l.143).
- R2-3: replace l.184 and l.426, and cut one of the two "the way…" similes (l.27 or l.64).
- R2-6: reconcile Buck's l.33 resolution with Ch5.
- R2-7: rephrase Maggie Mae's "I couldn't, at their age" (l.129).
- R2-8: split l.372 and l.411.

### Fact checks (Sherlock)

- **Rain-pipe mechanism (l.264, l.301):** sound. With the outlet submerged, water slugs falling down the pipe push the trapped air ahead of them, and it can only escape as bubbles through the basin water. That makes a periodic "glub". Benny's "Like blowing through a straw into a cup" is an accurate and very child-friendly analogy, better than r1's jug. Fine, subject to continuity item 4.
- **Slow drainage from a wet leaf mat in the gutter (l.309–311):** plausible.
- **Carolina wren (l.13):** accurate. It sings all year, it is a classic southern Appalachian bird, and its song is conventionally rendered "tea-kettle, tea-kettle".
- **Bald eagle "white chest" (l.123):** inaccurate (see R2-9). **Maverick "polishing his beak on a branch":** accurate (raptors wipe their bills on perches).

- **Chipmunk with an acorn and cheek pouches; hickory and maple colours; late roses; ladybird; frog on an autumn night; deer picking watercress:** accurate or plausible. Deer do browse watercress at stream edges.

- **Beaver "in the creek since dawn" (l.119):** species-appropriate.
