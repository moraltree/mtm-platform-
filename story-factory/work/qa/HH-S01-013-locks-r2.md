## Lock One

### "Piers Morgan", senior editor and literary critic

### Score: 8.6 / 10 — PASSED (pass mark 8.5), conditional on fixes R2-1 to R2-3 below

This revision is a real improvement, not a cosmetic one.

**The new close is now the best part of the story.**
- Buck at the buried steps learns the crackle from Charlie (l.430). "the trees putting on their nightshirts" is a lovely, timeless image.
- "I'll listen for it for you. Whenever you forget." (l.432) gives the child something to give the elder. That is exactly what Canon 2 means by Buck as the soul of the Hollow rather than its hero.
- #102 then lands on its own, with nothing paraphrasing it beforehand.

**Charlie's arc now adds a skill rather than taking something away.** The line is "I can do still now, Mama. As well as everything else" (l.393). Around it:
- Maggie Mae's "exactly enough Charlie… a fast Charlie on a slow day" (l.121), repeated quietly at the close (l.438);
- "Quickest eyes in the Hollow" (l.330);
- the bound into her mother's arms "which was exactly what everybody had expected her to do, and nobody minded at all" (l.395);
- the skipping hops her mother does not stop (l.440).

Together these fully answer the r1 concern about Canon 4.

**Beau now pays off 012 instead of contradicting it.** In l.231–235 he sets each paw down "as if every board were a footprint he had to read", then says "I'm not very good yet." It is funny, it fits his character, and it rewards a listener who heard 012.

**The ensemble is now pulling its weight.**
- Barney's attempt to climb over the rail, and Buck stopping him (l.332–334), is good safety modelling.
- Barney's morning-guess game (l.354–356, carried into l.438) gives him a real comic role.
- Maggie Mae hands Charlie on to Sadie Belle with "a look and a small nod" (l.121), which keeps the mentor bond intact.

**Why the score is not higher.**
- The Ch5–7 "wonders" stretch is still more a sequence than a build. The icicle faces (l.264) help, but the shape is still episodic.
- The underlying premise, slowness and stillness winning through Sadie Belle, is the third such story this season (006, 012). The 012 continuity turns that from repetition into a running thread, but it caps how fresh the story can feel.
- Three small mechanical slips remain. They are listed below as required fixes. Each takes a few words to fix.

### Remaining required fixes

- **R2-1. Window leftover (l.344).** "Ever since I knocked it out of the window" contradicts the new staging: Charlie shot between Benny's legs on the porch threshold, and the ruler skittered off the top step (l.95, l.326). Change it to, for example, "Ever since I knocked it off the step."
- **R2-2. Stove stool (l.103).** Charlie "climbed up on a stool to see better, and leaned, and leaned, and knocked the salt pot clean into the soup". That puts a child leaning over a hot pot on the stove, which is the image r1 S1 asked to remove. Keep the gag but move it to the table: for example, she climbs on a stool at the table beside Reba and knocks the salt pot into Reba's tasting bowl, or bumps the table so the salt pot rolls. Buck's line about the stove can stay as it is.
- **R2-3. Buried or dug-out steps.**
  - Ch9 says the miller dug a path "all the way to Buck's buried steps" (l.377), and Charlie bounds down "the miller's dug-out steps" (l.395).
  - Ch10 then has Buck at "the top of the buried steps" twice (l.418, l.434).
  - Choose one. The simplest fix is to have the miller clear the steps too, and change Ch10 to "the snowy steps" or "the top step".
  - Consider also a half-clause showing that the miller dug carefully round Benny's ruler, "a little to the left" of the steps (l.322). Otherwise a careful listener will wonder how Barney's morning-guess game survives the shovel.

### Advisory (not required)

- **A1.** Three grandparent sayings in one story: Sadie Belle's grandmother (l.272), Rusty's grandfather (l.301) and Buck's grandfather (l.430). Buck's is the best and should stay. Consider giving Rusty's line a different source ("that's what everybody calls them").
- **A2.** The ruler went in "end-first" into the morning's snow (l.326, l.342), so its reading includes however far it sank, not only "everything that had fallen since". Benny's "a better number than mine" already hedges this. It would read more precisely as "It had gone right down through the morning's snow to the step."
- **A3.** Sadie Belle turning to wave at the end of the path (l.438) echoes 012's littlest rabbit turning at the foot of the steps. It is minor and could be dropped.

### "Sherlock", natural history and physics

- **Springtails (l.281, l.287–301): now ACCURATE enough for fiction.**
  - The new staging is correct: a mild, soft hour after the storm, a thin-snow ring at the foot of the hickory where the brown leaves show through, insects at the edge where white meets leaf litter, "some of the tiniest", and the spring underneath.
  - One mild hour is a short window. Real emergences usually follow longer mild spells. But leaf litter showing through thin snow beside a trunk is exactly where they appear first, so this is acceptable.
- **Post-storm mild hour, then a clear cold night (l.281, l.416):** plausible. A lull with breaking cloud and low sun can feel soft before clear-sky cooling sets in after dark.
- **Tree crackle at nightfall (l.426–430):** plausible. As temperatures drop, wood contracts and branches loaded with frozen snow creak, crack and tick. Hard frost-cracking of trunks needs severe cold, but "a little crackle in the trees" is fine. "Snow sliding off the branches. Flump." fits the mild hour that has just passed.
- **Night-time chickadee call:** removed. The chickadees now "gone to roost" (l.360) is correct.
- **"Weighed almost nothing at all" (l.217):** fixed.
- **Ruler measurement:** acceptable. See advisory A2.
- **Unchanged and still accurate:** chipmunk torpor (l.5), chickadee identity, hand-feeding and seed-hammering (l.197–217), the cardinal in the holly (l.256), snow hush (l.170), frost ferns (l.270–272), icicle bubbles (l.264), willow snowshoes (l.379).
- **Icicle as a lens (l.264):** accurate. A clear icicle acts as a crude cylindrical lens, so the world seen through it looks distorted and, close up, inverted.
