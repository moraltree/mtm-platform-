## Lock One

### "Piers Morgan", senior editor and literary critic

### Score: 8.6 / 10 — PASSED (pass mark 8.5), conditional on the line-level fixes L1–L4 below

This is a real step up and, after small fixes, a publishable story. The comedy now builds: Ch4 is the funniest chapter in Season One so far. The honesty arc is cleaner because the frost fib is a single lapse with a clear cause. The fix belongs to the children, and the new idea, the sieve, comes from a discovery Reba made herself and Benny then turned into a tool. That is exactly what the brief meant by inventive problem-solving.

The ending is the best in the season. Reba walks home and tells her mother "the whole of it" under the bare hickories, and then says "Only my share." The honesty theme closes through action rather than a sermon, and the close is not recycled. I am holding back from a higher score for four reasons:
- One narrator moral survives (l.248).
- There is a doubled teamwork maxim.
- A small cluster of stock phrases remains.
- There is a new plot contradiction about Rusty's deliveries.

### Strengths
- **Ch4's escalation.** Each return tops the last, and each joke belongs to a character: Brody's patient bewilderment, the miller's doorpost, and Maggie Mae's "I thought so," said faintly. Beau's "It's… got a lot of flavour" is gentle, self-inflicted and kind. That is the right kind of joke for the Hollow.
- **The confession (Ch7)** is tighter and stronger. Buck's response is two lines and lands on gentle humour ("very glad not to be climbing that hill"). He does not solve anything.
- **Barney.** His silence now has a limit and a motive, and he states the safeguarding principle in his own voice and register: "Even nuts." The Float Tub Officer payoff and the second painted sign are neat.
- **The sieve.** Reba says "Smaller and rounder… That's how", Benny answers "Then we don't have to pick them out one by one", and "I measured it" is pure canon Benny. The miller's wife resting her paw on the sieve "as if she were thinking of keeping it" is a lovely, wordless approval.
- **Maverick's broadcast** is pure Maverick: brash, comic and loyal. "TOO LATE! BUT SHE'S VERY SORRY!" is a big laugh (see L3 and the Lock Three note on Reba's reaction).
- **Ch10.** The Mill supper uses a v1.0 location and the mill-wheel "rumbling… like somebody humming". Buck gives one line of wisdom and moves on ("Now, who'd like another tune?"). The doorstep scene with Reba's mother pays off Ch5's "When you can say, I'll be here." The mother's "Did you eat anything at that supper?" and Reba's "Only my share" is a perfect, small, funny, earned last exchange.

### Weaknesses
1. **The narrator still moralises (l.248):** "The trouble with a fib is that it does not stay where you put it." It is a good line, but it is the moral stated in the narrator's voice before Buck's #97. The chapter's next paragraph already shows the fib growing, so the line does nothing the story does not already do.
2. **The teamwork maxim is doubled (l.333, l.399).** Reba's "Unless everybody helps" is natural. The miller's wife's "That's what makes a week into an afternoon" is a second adult aphorism. Her preceding "So they did" is enough on its own.
3. **New contradiction: Rusty's deliveries (l.382 vs l.401–405).**
   - At l.338, "the whole Hollow brought its baskets back" to the shed.
   - Rusty then carries the right baskets "to the families who had gone home" (l.382).
   - Then Reba hands out baskets "at every one" at the shed (l.401). The rabbits, the miller, Brody and Maggie Mae all collect theirs in person (l.405–407).

   Rusty's job needs a clear group: for example, the families who could not come back, such as those along the far creek or the very old. Alternatively, he could fetch the remaining wrong baskets in, rather than deliver right ones out.
4. **Stock phrasing persists:**
   - "over her/his spectacles" (l.31, l.307)
   - "his whiskers twitched" (l.313)
   - "for a long moment" (l.229, l.307)
   - "paws in his pockets" twice (l.143, l.229)

   The spectacles look and the whiskers twitch now appear in nearly every S01 story.
5. **Small chronology hiccup in Ch3 (l.117).** Ch2 ends late in the afternoon with "Done!", and Ch3 then has Rusty arrive "Halfway through the afternoon". A listener hears time go backwards. A small fix is "Earlier that afternoon, Rusty had put his head round the door…" or moving Rusty's visit into Ch2.
6. **Ch1 still lacks a sensory "Sunrise on the Hollow" opening** (Bible §10.1). The story opens with exposition. This is advisory, not required: S01 has varied its openings, but this is one of the barer ones. A single sentence of autumn texture would help, and it would also add to the word count, which matters given the 5,046 margin.

### Required revisions (Lock One)
1. **L1.** Cut or recast l.248 as event, for example "By the next morning, the fib had grown." The paragraph after it already shows the growth.
2. **L2.** Trim l.399 to "So they did," said the miller's wife, followed by an action, such as a smile or her paw on the sieve.
3. **L3.** Resolve the contradiction between Rusty's deliveries and the handout (l.382 vs l.401–405).

**Word-count caution:** L1 and L2 remove about 15 words, which keeps the story above 5,000. The author should add rather than cut elsewhere, for example with the optional Ch1 sensory sentence.

### Fact checks (Sherlock)
- **Shagbark and bitternut description (l.11, l.364): now accurate.**
  - Shagbark: larger, with a thick, hard, four-ridged shell ("like a little box" is a good, child-friendly image).
  - Bitternut: smaller and rounder, with a thin, smooth shell and an intensely bitter kernel. It is never called poisonous. Keep it that way.
  - "Very hard to tell apart" when they are "tipped together in a heap, in a hurry, in a dim shed" is a fair qualification.
- **Weevils (l.13, l.358): accurate.** The larvae leave through a small round exit hole. Charlie's "Every empty one's got a tiny hole!" is a slight over-generalisation: some empty nuts simply never filled and have no hole. Optional: "nearly every one". It is harmless as a child's discovery, and the float tub still catches the unholed empties.
- **Float test: accurate.** Drying is now included (l.360), and Sadie Belle's "So they don't go mouldy" is correct. **Note:** an afternoon in the sun dries the surface, but soaked hickory nuts really need several days spread out before storage. Optional fix: "and told every family to spread them out at home for a few days more". That is one sentence, and it helps the word count.
- **Benny's size-sieve. This is a new fact issue and required as a one-line fix.** Bitternut and shagbark sizes **overlap**. Small shagbarks would drop through with the bitternuts, which only loses a few sweet nuts. Large bitternuts would stay on top with the shagbarks, and bitter nuts in the sweet baskets are exactly the original problem. In practice, a size grading does the bulk of the work but needs a hand check. Add one line in which Reba runs her paws over what stays on top, feeling for the four ridges, and picks out the odd round, smooth one. This also gives Reba's newly learned skill a final job.
- **Nut-stone and hammer-stone (l.70):** correct traditional method for hard hickory shells.
- **Drilling a plank with even holes in one afternoon, with the miller using a mill tool:** plausible (a brace and bit).
- **Mill-wheel turning at evening (l.434):** plausible for atmosphere, though a working mill wheel at night is unusual. Acceptable as a v1.0-canon sound ("one of the abiding sounds of the Hollow").
- **Mountain Wisdom #97** is verbatim. The round-1 "young'uns" insertion has been removed.

This is now a **model treatment**:

That final scene is excellent modelling. It shows a child that telling a parent afterwards is safe and welcome.
