## Lock One

### "Piers Morgan", senior editor and literary critic

### Score: 8.4 / 10 — NOT YET PASSED (pass mark 8.5; very close)

This is now the best-constructed story of the season. The mystery is solved by the children and the friends together, each through a character-true gift. That is exactly what Bible §10.5 asks for, and it reads naturally, not as a checklist. It has several new moments that earn their place:
- **Barney naming the pegs** ("Then it's not waiting. It's meeting people."; Sir Earlybird, Mrs Frostypaws, and a peg called Benny). Comic, in character, and it dramatises patience.
- **The mother's bedside scene** (l.315–329). "I can hear you worrying from the kitchen" and "You know what you know." It is tender and grounded, and it hands the confidence back to the child rather than solving anything.
- **The wonders ring (Ch9).** It is varied, specific and funny: Reba's jam "from two summers ago", Barney's "rarer than any sunrise". It is quietly moving too: Brody's frog singing "the note his banjo could never quite reach", and Maverick saying his "not showing off at all". Beau's heron wonder lands the moral without stating it. Sadie Belle's "I saw a white stone in the grass. Just one. And I wondered why it was there." is a perfect closing line for the theme of curiosity.
- **The whittled wren** as the closing object: set up in Ch4 (l.146), carried through Ch7 (l.295), paid off in Ch10. That is good craft.
- **Buck admitting he was cross in the dark** (l.446). This mirrors Beau and makes Buck human, not oracular.

### What keeps it below 8.5
1. **The central count contradicts itself (Ch5–6).** After the third peg, Benny counts "Seven more claws. Seven more mornings." (l.205). But the sixth peg lands "just one claw's width from the notch" (l.248) and Benny says "Tomorrow" (l.252). By his own figure the sixth peg should be four claws away and the event four days off. The mother's "every morning for a week" (l.323) matches the seven-day version, not the ten-day one. In a story whose whole point is careful measuring, this is the one number that must be right. An attentive parent reading aloud will catch it. See R2-2.
2. **Buck's defining line about Halfway Morning is astronomically wrong (l.360).** See Lock Two. It is also a craft problem, because it is the story's key revelation.
3. **The evening/afternoon inversion** (l.250 → l.273) recurs.
4. **Two blushes with the same mist device** (l.82, l.403) still read as formula. The second, "was very glad that there was still a little mist on the creek", also repeats round 1's "once again" structure in spirit.
5. **The Wisdom is pre-explained** (l.446; see R2-6).
6. **Minor wording.** "The sky turned from black to deep blue" appears twice (l.164, l.344). "Brilliant" appears four times. Beau falling asleep chin-to-chest echoes S01-001. "Nobody said a single word." / "Nobody said anything after that." are stock, but acceptable.

With R2-1 to R2-6 done, I would expect this to score about 8.7–8.8.

### Required revisions (Lock One)
- **R2-2.** Fix Benny's count. For example, after the third peg: "Four," he said. "Four more claws. Four more mornings." (Beau's "That's ages" still works.) Or keep "seven" and move the first peg further back, rewriting Ch5–6 so the arithmetic holds. Check the mother's "every morning for a week" (l.323) against whatever total results.
- **R2-3.** Fix the time order: change "That evening, Benny sat…" (l.250) to "That afternoon" or "After breakfast". This also avoids Benny sitting alone at the creek in the evening.
- **R2-5.** Keep one blush. Either cut the mist clause at l.403 or vary that beat.
- **R2-6.** Trim l.446 to end at "And then the sun came up in the notch." Let #41 do the saying.
