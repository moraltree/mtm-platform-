### "Sherlock", natural history and physics

The round 1 fixes are verified as correct:
- **S-1 (old):** the snow is now dug and barrowed by grown-ups under a clear sky (l.384), and the night stays clear (l.399). The confound I raised in round 1 is removed.
- **S-4:** paddle ice is knocked off each morning and the wheel runs slowly all day so the spray "never had time to build" (l.450). This is sound practice.
- **S-5:** snow keeps burrows "warmer than the air" (l.294). Correct.
- **S-7:** six turns a minute throughout, suitably "slow and patient" (Bible).
- **The new Ch4 physics is right.** A wet model freezes within an hour in a bitter frost. Warm water melts the surface ice and then refreezes on it, leaving more ice than before (l.180). Noise does not shift ice (l.186). Snow sliding off a shed roof when it is jarred is plausible.
- **Charlie's "stopped steaming"** (l.388) is correct: once the water surface is covered, no visible steam fog can form above it.
- Frazil, ice-cover insulation, historical race covering and wheel imbalance are unchanged and correct.

**Required**
- **S-1 (new). The sluice is in the wrong place for the story's logic.** At l.54 the sluice is "a wooden gate at the top end of the race". When it is down, the water "goes off round the side, down the spillway… back into the creek". At l.401 the sluice is down overnight. That means the race below the gate carries no flowing water overnight, so:
  - (a) the covered race is never actually tested that night (no flow, no frazil either way), and the cover cannot be credited for the result;
  - (b) l.421 ("Dark, quick, moving water, running clear through the grate") contradicts a closed gate at the top.

  In a real mill the controlling sluice (penstock or shuttle) sits at the wheel end, after the trash rack. With the gate down, the race keeps flowing through the grate and out by the bypass or spillway, and the wheel stands still. That is exactly what the story needs. Word-neutral fix (R1):
  - l.54: "a wooden gate at the top end of the race" → "a wooden gate at the far end of the race, just past the grate". Then "Up, and the water comes down the race to the wheel." → "Up, and the water drops onto the wheel."
  - l.427: "pouring down the covered race and through the grate" → "pouring out under the gate".
  - l.401 and l.421 then read correctly as written.

**Recommended (unchanged from round 1, not required)**
- S-6: give the covered bucket "a thin rim of ice" and say the buckets were filled from the pump.
- S-8: either "It's a very long race" (l.294) or thirty boards that fit "right across the top" (l.333, 358). One should give.
