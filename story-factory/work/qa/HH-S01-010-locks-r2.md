### "Piers Morgan", senior editor and literary critic

### Score: 8.4 / 10 — NOT YET PASSED (pass mark 8.5; very close)

This is a substantial and intelligent revision. The story now has a fresh mechanism (an heirloom star-tin whose old holes have rusted shut, so the sky has to be "made again"). Its comedy escalates on stage, and in Ch6 Maverick genuinely chooses to wait, which dramatises the brief's harder half. Buck is a real principal without solving anything. The Ch8 image of the littlest rabbits catching stars on their paws, one cross-eyed with the Little Lanterns on her nose (l.384–390), is the most original moment of the season. The Rocking Chair W/M quarrel is a perfect child-sized science joke that resolves itself correctly.

It stops at 8.4 for three reasons:
- House rule 1 is still breached at the close (Maverick's summary and Buck's lead-in).
- Ch2 runs in a confusing time order for a listener.
- There are a handful of line-level slips: one banned-phrase variant, a snowdrift pratfall echoing 009, and Reba's recycled "only ate four" gag.

All of these are line fixes. With R2-1 to R2-5 done I would expect a pass.

### Strengths

### Facts ("Sherlock")

- **F-r2-1 (must fix, one word): the Little Lanterns' direction (l.425).** I recalculated for 35.6°N in early December with the Belt just risen in the east. The Pleiades stand about 35–45° above it at nearly the same azimuth, a few degrees further north. Facing east, that puts them **up and slightly to the left**, not to the right. Round 1's "up and to the left" was correct. Change it to "High up above the Stepping Stones, a little to the left" or simply "A good way straight up from the Stepping Stones".
- **F-r2-2 (wording): the crescent (l.413).** On my day count the moon is about 4–5 days old on the clear night: a slim crescent (about 15–25% lit), about 20–25° up in the WSW at 7 pm, setting at about 8:30–9 pm. "So new and thin that it was barely a silver thread", sinking out of sight "in a little while" in "early evening", describes a 1–2-day moon. Fixes:
  - (a) say "a slim young crescent… and by the time Maverick had finished naming the bright ones it had slipped down behind Weather Ridge"; or
  - (b) shorten the cloud so the sky clears on the first or second night after the dark moon.

  Also make Star Night's definition match: "the first clear night once the moon has gone dark", so that a crescent setting early in the evening does not contradict the custom's "no moon at all" (l.5).
- **The waning moon (l.21–25):** a crescent four nights before new moon rises in the small hours, in the east ✓.
- **The Rocking Chair (Cassiopeia):** circumpolar, high in the north above Polaris on early-December evenings ✓. "Five bright stars in a crooked zigzag" ✓. It appears as an M when above Polaris and a W when below. It turns round the pole through the night, so Benny's explanation (l.317) is correct ✓. The traditional figure is a chair, a charming match ✓.
- **The Porch Star:** steady in the north ✓. "Find your way home by it" is folk lore (it gives direction, not home); acceptable.
- **The Three Stepping Stones:** "just rising over Sunrise Ridge, low and bright" in early evening. In early December the Belt rises about 7 pm at this latitude ✓, consistent with "early in the evening". The sword "hanging down from them" ✓ (the middle "star" is the Orion Nebula, a soft patch; averted vision helps) ✓.
- **The Little Lanterns:** six seen, seven on good nights ✓. Only the direction is wrong (F-r2-1).
- **Averted vision** (l.301, l.370, l.423) ✓ correct: rods sit off the fovea.
- **Fox night vision:** tapetum lucidum ("a sort of mirror at the back") ✓. Eyeshine green to yellowish, seen near a light source ✓.
- **Bald eagle:** diurnal and very sharp by day. Low-light sensitivity is poor relative to nocturnal mammals ✓. "Better than anybody at dusk" is a boast, not narration; acceptable.
- **Cloud disorientation;** gliding until the ground appears ✓.
- **F-r2-3 (should fix): the star-tin's optics.**
  - The principle is sound: a pierced-tin lantern throws soft points of light, and bigger holes give brighter points ✓.
  - Problem: a drum "open at the top" with a chimneyed lamp inside will throw a large, bright disc of direct light onto the low ceiling straight above it. That washes out the ceiling stars in that area and lifts the whole room's light level. Real pierced-tin lanterns have pierced, vented lids for this reason.
  - Fix: Buck sets a pierced tin lid on top, raised on little pegs so the heat can get out. Or acknowledge it charmingly: "a soft round glow on the ceiling right above it, and all around that, the stars". Note that a "glow like the moon" would undercut the moonless theme.
  - Also say that the charcoal paper is taken off the tin before the lamp goes in (l.291 to l.350).
  - The cylinder rather than a sphere distorts the outer stars slightly; that is fine for a story.
- **Weather** (low winter stratus for a week, flurries, slow thinning) ✓. **A heron on the winter creek** ✓.
