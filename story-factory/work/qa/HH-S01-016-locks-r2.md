### "Sherlock", natural history and physics

**Verified (new or changed material):**
- **Hepatica flowers last "a week or two"** (l.25). Individual flowers last about a week and a colony blooms over two or three weeks, so this is fine.
- **Bloodroot "because of its root... the juice inside is orange-red"** (l.185) is correct. The root and stem both bleed, and the sap irritates the skin ("stings", l.195).
- **Bloodroot flowers close at night** (l.418) is correct. They close in the evening and in dull weather.
- **Red maples flowering in mid-March** (l.390) is correct for the southern Appalachians, and "hazed with little red flowers" is apt.
- **Spring azures** ("tiny pale-blue butterflies", l.390) are correct as a classic early-spring butterfly.
- **Hepaticas "going over" by mid-March, with a few late ones open** (l.388), is correct.
- **Trailing arbutus under an oak** (l.218) is a good habitat match for acidic, well-drained soil.
- **The scarlet cup fungus on a fallen stick in early March** (l.78) is correct (scarlet elf cup).
- **A bee on hepatica in early March** (l.344) is correct.
- **A barred owl calling at dusk in mid-March** (l.420) is correct. They are vocal in late winter and early spring.
- **Watercolour practice** (l.348, l.400): a brush, a water jar, white paint and drying between sheets of clean paper (l.416) are all fine.

**Required fix:**
- **S-1 (l.418).** "and the grey-green leaf that had been its cloak curled round it again". The bloodroot leaf is wrapped round the flower bud only as the plant first comes up. It unfurls as the flower opens and keeps expanding afterwards. It does not re-wrap the flower each evening, although in cold, dull weather a young leaf may stay partly furled. Cut the clause, or replace it with "and its grey-green leaf stood quietly beside it, like a folded cloak". Keep the flower closing, which is accurate.

**Advisory:**
- **S-2 (l.56).** Benny's "Flowers can't come up through snow" is not strictly true, but it stands as a child's reasoning (unchanged from r1).
- **S-3 (l.400).** Painting white petals on white paper: a "Chinese white" in an old paintbox makes this plausible, and the puddle comedy is consistent with it.

**Sherlock result: PASS WITH NOTES** (conditional on S-1).
