### "Sherlock", natural history and physics

**Verdict: PASSED, with one should-fix.**

#### Verified in revision

- **Fritillary.**
  - A near-mature larva in late May, "nearly as long as Reba's finger", is correct.
  - Prepupal hanging and stopping feeding in the first week of June is correct.
  - "a few weeks" to the adult, "at the start of summer", is correct for the southern Appalachians.
- **Spicebush swallowtail.**
  - Laying in hot, mid-morning sun is correct.
  - The hosts are spicebush and sassafras, which is correct.
  - Single eggs on separate twigs are correct.
  - The first week of June fits a late first-brood or early second-brood female.
- **Ruby-throated hummingbird.**
  - It visits glossy, red, tubular, unscented flowers and ignores bees' flowers, which is correct; the flowers are hummingbird-pollinated.
  - The shining green back and the throat that "flashed red when it turned" are correct for a male's gorget.
  - Hovering, a wing "blur and a hum", and a long thin beak into each tube are correct.
  - Feeding in the evening before dark is correct; hummingbirds feed heavily before roosting.
  - Exploring a newly placed red pot is plausible.
- **Bees and scent.** Reba's hedged "So they go by smell. Partly." (l.170) is honest: bees use scent, colour and shape. "it wasn't the colour" is a child's over-confident inference within a hedged sentence, so it is acceptable.
- **Divisions** of bee balm and coneflower flowering the same summer are correct. Milkweed "might take till next year" is correct. "first tight buds just showing" in early June is plausible for established divisions.
- **The last of the blackberry blossom** in early June is correct.
- **Chimney swifts chittering** is correct.

#### Should fix

- **S-1. Hummingbird size (l.170).** "no bigger than my thumb" is too small: a ruby-throated hummingbird is about 7–9 cm long, and a raccoon's thumb is a few centimetres. Use "no bigger than my paw", or "hardly bigger than a bumblebee's big cousin" for a whimsical version.

#### Optional

- **Fritillary pupation site (l.312, l.351).** "hanging upside down from the undersides of the violet leaves" is a picture-book simplification. Real pupation is low down, in leaf litter or a loose leaf shelter. "low down among the violet leaves" is equally pretty and exact.
