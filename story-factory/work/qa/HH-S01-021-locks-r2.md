## New or remaining issues found in round 2

**N-1. Beau's retelling contradicts the revised Ch3 (l.305–311). REQUIRED.** Beau tells Granny Opal that "nobody could get across at all until Charlie went underneath", and when she asks "Underneath?", he says "Along the beam … Like a squirrel. And then my hat fell in the stream … Barney fished it out with a stick." None of this now happens in Ch3:
- Charlie steps over a "wet line in the grass" upstream (l.137);
- the hat rolls into the mud (l.143);
- Barney "fetched it back" (l.143).

This is the comic retelling that makes Opal laugh, so it is a high-visibility error. It also puts back into the narration the two images Dr Noura asked to be removed: a child climbing under a bridge, and fishing in a stream with a stick.

Suggested fix, keeping the "Underneath?" rhythm as a question and answer:
- "…until Charlie just walked up the bank and stepped over."
- Opal: "Stepped over?"
- Beau: "Where it's only a wet line in the grass. We could all have done it. Hours ago." He scooped up more flour. "And then my hat rolled in the mud. It was my dad's hat. Barney had to fetch it."

**N-2. Morning departure against Buck's evening remark (l.3–9). REQUIRED.** "It started with something Great-Grandpappy Buck said, on the evening after the Blossom Gathering" (l.3). But the visitors "had gone home … that morning" (l.5), and Buck speaks "watching him go" (l.7), with "Everybody on the porch nodded" (l.9). The badger cannot be leaving in the morning while Buck watches him go that evening. Choose one:
- **(a)** l.3 becomes "on the morning after the Blossom Gathering". Buck says it at the edge of Fern Meadow, or from the porch as the badger goes, and l.15 becomes "He thought about it all day … and all night, too". This means the lessons start on Monday only if the Gathering was on Saturday and this is Sunday morning, which works.
- **(b)** Keep the evening, and have Buck say it *remembering* the morning's leave-taking: "That old badger this morning," said Buck, rocking. "Now there goes a true gentleman."

Option (b) is lighter, and it keeps the porch opening.

**N-3. Maggie Mae "heard about" a party she attended (l.436). REQUIRED.** She sat at the end watching her cups (l.233), so "I heard about your tea party" no longer makes sense. Suggested fix: "Well," she said, "*that* was a tea party." Or drop the line and go straight to "My cups survived. Nearly all of them." The cups line already carries the comic callback.

**N-4. Beau sits down twice (l.403–405). REQUIRED (minor).** "…and Beau sat down beside her and did not notice either thing." is followed by "Beau sat down. Nobody stood up." Cut the second "Beau sat down." so the paragraph opens "Nobody stood up. Nobody bowed."

**N-5. Close wording (l.421, l.450, l.456–458). REQUIRED (minor).**
- "Did not seem to mind them at all" (l.421) and "nobody minded at all" (l.450) still echo 011 (round 1 R-10). Change one, for example l.450 to "and everybody looked ridiculous in it, Buck included", if Buck takes a turn, or simply end the sentence at "looked ridiculous in it".
- "Then, slowly, the porch grew quiet" (l.456), followed by "Buck rocked slowly in his chair" (l.458), sits close to the banned "first slow creak … the porch went quiet". Strictly applied, rephrase: "Then, little by little, the talk went soft and low."

The parents with lanterns (l.456) are a familiar S01 shape (002, 010, 012). They are acceptable as safety staging. The elderberry-scented neckerchief and Opal's laugh are what make this close fresh.
