## Lock One

### "Piers Morgan", senior editor and literary critic

### Score: 8.6 / 10 — PASSED (pass mark 8.5), subject to the line fixes below

The revision is faithful to the r1 list, and it improves the story rather than just complying with it. The three biggest gains:
- **Rusty is now a true co-principal.** He fetches the mother (l.363–365), stands cheering at the bottom of the hill (l.388), gets a staged congratulation (l.418–422), and the refrain resolves: "We saw." / "We did." (l.495–497). This is economical and a good payoff.
- **Mountain Wisdom #2 fits the story far better than #50.** Buck's anecdote (pulling his little sister's sledge up and missing the race, l.514–518) dramatises the saying without summarising it. It ends on the sister ("Laughing all the way"), and the saying lands cleanly and word for word (l.520). The rhyme with Beau walking the littlest rabbit up the hill is felt, not explained.
- **The close is fresh.** Benny's deadpan final results ("Nose-first leaps: one, not counted… I'm going to need a bigger notebook", l.532) are the funniest line in the story. The last image, the roll-marks with "one very small one, which went on, and on" (l.536), is original in the series and quietly lovely.

Also improved: the cocoa-pot trail and "Lost, dear?" (l.243–249) is a better gag than the borrowed Woozle. "Ta-da," said Rusty politely (l.39) is a deft swap for the "meant to" beat. Maverick checking the run-out (l.446–448) is useful, in character and funny ("looking extremely dignified"). The whole-meadow apology and Brody's "That's all right, Beau" (l.430–434) is a good community beat. Beau cheering "Three on the head!" in scene (l.465) puts the sportsmanship on stage.

House rules: rule 1 is met for the saying, with one narrator lapse (fix 5 below). Rules 2–4 and 6 are met. Rule 5 is met: no banned close elements, and none of the banned phrases appear on a grep. The lantern walk home is functional rather than a closing image. The littlest rabbit falling asleep on her father's shoulders (l.530) is a single child, not a "falling asleep one by one" sequence, so it is acceptable.

Why not higher: a cluster of small continuity slips was introduced by the revision itself (below), and the littlest rabbit still carries a large share of the emotional climax for a non-cast character (20 mentions). That last point is now acceptable because Rusty and the ensemble share the turn.

### Required line fixes (none is structural)

1. **Throwing distance.** Benny marks the line "twenty of Benny's paces" (l.46), but l.111 says "thirty paces". Make them agree.
2. **Two red mittens.** The tracking prize is "a red mitten" that Sadie Belle has already carried out of the woods (l.237, l.251). In the Roll-Down Reba finds "a red mitten" lost by the miller's wife (l.450). Listeners will think it is the same mitten. Change one (the miller's wife's could be a blue mitten or a knitted scarf), and if possible plant her losing it earlier (a one-clause mention at the cocoa table, l.247, would do).
3. **Charlie's rosette in Ch8.** Rosettes are now all given out at the end of the day (l.50, Ch9), but at l.398 Charlie stands "with her FIRST rosette". Change to "with Maggie Mae" or "still holding the walnut-sized snowball she had saved". (Rusty's "She won her first ever ribbon", l.306, is acceptable as "won", but "first ever event" would be cleaner.)
4. **The leftover Woozle line.** Beau tells Sadie Belle "I went round in a circle following myself" (l.426). The gag is now the cocoa-pot trail. For example: "I followed a cocoa pot."
5. **The narrator labels the lesson after the saying, and repeats itself.** l.493 says Beau "had never in his whole life been so pleased with anything"; l.522 says "He had not won a single thing all day. He had never in his life been so pleased with a day." The second spells out the moral straight after Buck's saying (house rule 1) and duplicates the first. Cut the last two sentences of l.522 and let the image of the long slow walks stand.
6. **The snowball's path.** Beau's third throw "went sideways, sailed over the heads of the watchers behind the line, and knocked the miller's hat clean off" (l.137). A sideways throw cannot pass over watchers standing behind the thrower. Make it fly high and wild and come down on the hat of the miller, who is standing well to the side. Keep the watchers behind the line.

Remaining continuity items: fixes 1–4 in Lock One (distance, mittens, Charlie's rosette, the circle line). Two minor ones that are optional but recommended:

### "Sherlock", facts and plausibility

All r1 fact items are met: F1 roly-poly bug, F2 mild packing snow (l.3, l.105), F3 Charlie at a nearer mark with walnut-sized snowballs, F5 "Whether they were or not" with the tail as rudder. The holly is gone.

One new fact problem, introduced by the revision:
- **F6. The knot scale is far too small.** The knots are tied "every paw's width" (l.46). Sadie Belle's leap is "nine knots and a half" and Rusty's "ten knots" (l.168–170). Ten paw-widths of a fox or beaver is roughly half a metre, and a running young deer or fox clears far more than that. Fix: a knot every "tail's length", or "every stride of Benny's", or keep the paw-width but make the numbers much bigger ("forty knots and a half"). Benny's final results line (l.532) must change to match.

## Lock Three

### "Dr Noura", safeguarding, bedtime suitability and narration readiness

*This is an AI role-based assessment, not clinical or professional safeguarding advice.*

All six r1 conditions are met:
- **S1.** Beau says "we have to ask your mother first. That's the rule" (l.361), and the mother listens, agrees and watches; every run is "never higher than her mother allowed" (l.388). This is a model beat.
- **S2.** "I'll walk right beside you"; she sits facing forward, feet first (l.375–377), and Beau's cheering repeats it ("Sit up tall! Feet first!", l.390).
- **S3.** The Roll-Down has no sledges and is on the flat foot of the slope, one at a time, approved by Buck, with Maverick checking for sticks and stones and Brody stamping a flat start. There is no backwards riding. The deliberate-falling imitation risk is gone; rolling on soft, checked, flat snow is a safe game.
- **S4.** The soft-snow line is qualified (l.355). The chin-tuck, arms-in, roll technique (l.369) is sound general fall-safety advice in this setting.
- **S5.** No holly; Beau packs his snowball big, not hard; watchers stand behind the line; there is an immediate muffled voice after the plunge (l.184); Maggie Mae helps tie the rosette by its ribbons, with no pin (l.491).
- **S6.** "No good" replaces "stupid", the Barney and Benny put-downs are folded into one, every hurt friend gets an on-page repair, and Beau apologises to the whole meadow.

Adults walk every group home with lanterns (l.534), and the littlest rabbit is carried home asleep. The wind-down is good: Ch10 lowers the energy steadily, the laughter is "softly, because it was late", and the final image is still. Shouting in capitals is confined to Ch7–8. Dialogue attribution is clear throughout.

Notes (not conditions):
- **Bumping into Brody.** Beau "bumped gently into Brody at the bottom" of the Roll-Down (l.452). Buck's own rule is that nobody starts till the one before has walked off. Have Beau stop "just short of Brody's boots", or have Brody stand aside.
- **The table.** The littlest rabbit climbs on the table "which she was not supposed to do" (l.475) and is not corrected. It is harmless, but a two-word "and climbed down again" or Maggie Mae lifting her down would avoid rewarding it.
- **Read-aloud.** "The littlest rabbit" now appears 20 times. Varying a few ("the little one", "she") would ease the narrator's voice; naming her is a founder decision.
- **Snowball fix 6 in Lock One** also removes a muddled image of a snowball passing over the watchers' heads.
