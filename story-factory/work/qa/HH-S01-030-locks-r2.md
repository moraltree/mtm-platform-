## LOCK ONE: "Piers Morgan" (story quality)

**Score: 8.6 / 10. PASS (pass mark 8.5)**

### Strengths
- **The Wisdom now lands as a revelation, not a summary.** The story never says #79. It *shows* it: "the whole Hollow had come out to meet them coming home" (l.236), with Buck waiting at the end of the bridge. Buck's line (l.361, verbatim §8 #79, unused in 001–029) then names what the listener has just watched happen. That is exactly what Rule 1 is for.
- **Buck's memory (l.357) is the best fix in the revision.** "A big old paw resting on his shoulder… His grandpappy's paw. He could still feel the weight of it." It silently rhymes with Rusty's father's paw on Barney's shoulder at the lip (l.158). It carries the generational meaning with no theme stated, and it gives Buck a moment of private feeling before he speaks.
- **Barney's hesitation (l.215–221) gives the promise weight.** He looks back towards the river he can no longer see, "not pointing at anything", then looks at Charlie's face and nods. Wanting, cost and a choice are all shown in three sentences. Barney's "not till Charlie's bigger than me" / "That's never" / "Exactly" (l.338–344) then turns the same promise into a laugh. That is a fine finale tonal turn, and Charlie gets the punchline herself, delighted.
- **The close works as the season's last image.**
  - The leaf planted in Ch6 is loosened by the wind of Ch7, falls past the rail of Grandpappy's Bridge in Ch10 and floats away "towards… the far silver river".
  - The brothers watch it, "did not follow it", and stay "in the middle of the Hollow".
  - It hints at Canon 5's downstream journey without a log, a departure or any imitation risk, which honours founder default 1 precisely.
  - It avoids walk-home lanterns, "X looked back", a glowing object as the final image, and falling asleep.
- **Comedy is better staged.** The drum's double "No" (l.70–76), Reba's three lunches paid off at lunch (l.78, l.129), Beau's "Round. Not over." and his trip into the ferns going round (l.103–107), and Rusty's "but not today" (l.111). Granny Opal's "You will be" (l.328) is a perfect dry line. For a reflective finale, this is the right amount.
- **The set pieces are intact:** the reveal at the edge (l.140–148) and the north wind (Ch7). The new paragraph at l.150 (the roar, the cool breath off the spray, two specks circling, a far bell) deepens the sense of a world beyond without any menace.
- **Ensemble:** all eleven are present and each does in-character work:
  - Sadie Belle finds the deer path and her lobelia;
  - Maverick scouts and reconciles 020;
  - Benny maps and draws;
  - Brody is a step, carries Charlie, and promises the tune;
  - Maggie Mae brings the lunch and holds Charlie's paw;
  - Rusty reads the shortcuts but holds back;
  - Charlie asks the question and makes the bravery promise;
  - Reba and Beau supply the comedy.
- **Rules 6 and 2 are clean.** There are no banned phrases and zero "the way X does" similes. Adults ask no questions ("It's a sight, isn't it." at l.244 is a remark).

### Remaining weaknesses (not blocking unless noted)
1. **The ice "job" (l.195, l.336).** This is a canon and safety error, not a quality one, and it is required under Locks Two and Three (R2-1). It is also the *only* concrete reason Benny gives for staying, so its replacement should be equally concrete and in character for Benny the builder.
2. **l.353, "and that seemed to say it well enough."** Now that Ch8 ends on the lanterns rather than on the promises, "it" has no clear referent, and it is a silence beat directly before the Wisdom. Cut the clause: "For a while after that, there was only the rain, pattering softly on the leaves above them."
3. **Consecutive bridge-rail closes.** 029 ends on Charlie's fans on the rail of Grandpappy's Bridge, and 030 ends with the brothers at the same rail. The images, characters and action differ, and the bridge is the Hollow's "meeting place" in the Bible, so this is acceptable. Note it for the season register so that S02's opener does not end there too.
4. **Adverbs.** "Gently" ×5, "softly" ×4, "slowly" ×4, "quietly" ×4. That is tolerable, but trim two or three for the narrator.

### Sherlock: verdict PASS

- **Great blue lobelia:** "tall spikes of deep, bright blue, standing up in the shade" (l.113), in a damp shady hollow in early September. Correct.
- **The early-turning leaf:** "after a hot dry summer" (l.264) now ties Buck's lore to the real mechanism, drought stress causing early yellowing in individual leaves. Hickory butter-gold is correct. A stressed leaf letting go in the last breath of wind (l.384) is plausible.
- **Leaf geometry:** the old hickory beside Buck's lodge has branches over the creek (l.254, l.256), and Grandpappy's Bridge is near the lodge (Bible), so a leaf from the furthest branch falling past the rail (l.386) works.
- **Creek recovery:** "a little trickle… began to grow" (l.297), "Not much. Not yet… a little creek again" (l.374), and Benny's "It'll take a lot more rain than this to fill it" (l.376). Plausible and well judged.
- **The cool north front with soft rain and no thunder** (Ch7), easing to mist by nightfall (l.368). Sound.
- **The falls' "soft, steady roar… even with so little water"** and the cool breath off the spray (l.150). Plausible for a cascade, even at low flow. The "two big birds turning slow circles" are soaring raptors or vultures on thermals in the afternoon. Neutral, with no predation. **The faint single bell** heard across miles of valley on still air is possible, and the text hedges it ("could not be sure you had heard it at all").
- **Timing:** the edge in the early afternoon, staying "until the sun had moved a good way across the sky" (l.168), and back "in the late afternoon" (l.232). That is tight but feasible against a sunset of about 7.45 pm. "Well before dark" holds.
- **Wording advisory (l.150):** "from some place none of them had ever heard of" is logically odd, because they cannot tell which place it is. Prefer "from somewhere none of them had ever been".
