## Lock One — "Piers Morgan", senior editor and literary critic

### Score: 8.3 / 10 — NOT YET A PASS (pass mark 8.5; close)

The revision is a real improvement. The mechanism is now planted and paid off, the children solve the problem, and the ensemble earns its place. Nothing structural remains. What holds it below 8.5 is one new mechanical contradiction at the climax, a new close that recycles S01-003's final image and steps away from the porch ritual without a sign-off, and a residue of stock phrasing the r1 report listed explicitly.

### Strengths
- **The set-up and payoff now work as a machine** a child can follow:
  - Ch3: Buck shows the loop and warns ("Always keep it looped").
  - Ch3: the wind gives Brody a reason to close the doors.
  - Ch3: the sack flicks the loop off unnoticed.
  - Ch3–4: clunk.
  - Ch5: the rain-barrel is mentioned in passing.
  - Ch6: the hole is behind it.

  This is genuine craft, not patching.
- **The children own the solution.** Benny's "She doesn't have to push it" is a satisfying click. Buck's silence about the plan is pointed and in character.
- **The ensemble sings in Ch7–8:**
  - Maverick's eagle-eyed safety scout and "Left! Round the pumpkins!"
  - Reba's wrist-string "so you've got something to hold".
  - Barney's crossed-eyes face and the Latch-String Jig "that nobody else could follow, because he kept changing it" (the best new joke).
  - Rusty whistling "every birdsong he knew at once".
  - Reba's bruised apples paying off the Ch2 gag.
- **Brody's guilt is held well.** "It's my fault," then "That'll mend", then Sadie Belle's "He told me once about a whole bridge" / "Brody almost smiled". This is touching and economical.
- **The Ch10 image of Brody humming** so that Charlie "felt the humming go right through her, from his big chest into her small back, like a cat purring" is the best new line in the book, and it folds the Ch7 thread back into the lullaby.
- **The frost logic is now honest:** apples tolerate it, squash and potatoes do not.

### Weaknesses
1. **Door direction contradicts itself at the climax (Ch4 vs Ch8).**
   - Ch4: Brody "put both paws on the handle and pulled" to open the doors, and Buck warns he'll "pull them off their frame". The doors open outward.
   - Ch8: "Buck put his paw against the door and pushed, and the great doors… swung slowly open." From outside, that means they open inward.
   - Inward-swinging doors would also sweep into the hay and potato crates just inside, and into a barn "so full" that sacks had to be moved.

   An attentive adult will catch it at the story's key moment.
2. **The close leaves the porch ritual without a recorded sign-off (Ch10).** Canon 3 (v1.0) calls the porch-and-rocking-chair close "inviolable". The Season One founder default softens this to "porch-and-wisdom close in most stories, staging varied". The barn-step close with frost, stars, lanterns and Mountain Wisdom #16 is warm and arguably justified. But it is the first non-porch close of the season, and it drops the rocking chair's first slow creak (Bible §10.9's "signal" to the listener). It needs an explicit founder decision rather than an author's choice. (Buck "rocked a little where he sat" on a stone step reads oddly without the chair.)
3. **The new ending recycles S01-003's.** In S01-003, sleepy Charlie is delivered to her mother at night, who comes "with a candle in her paw". In S01-005, sleeping Charlie is handed to her mother, who comes "with a candle-lantern". Two of five closes now end on the same picture.
4. **Stock phrasing listed in r1 survives** (six phrases; see the revision check, item 8), plus Sadie and Beau's "close but not too close" and "bow over one ear". This is the main thing keeping the prose below publishable polish.
5. **Small logic gaps:**
   - Ch4: "we haven't hung it out for years, because we keep the doors open at harvest." The barn is used year-round (Bible v1.0: feasts, music evenings), so harvest week does not explain years of disuse. A simpler line works better: "It's been hanging inside since we opened up for harvest, and nobody thought to put it out."
   - Ch6: Charlie's "Not if Brody couldn't shift the doors" is a non sequitur (shifting doors is not lifting a latch). A child might say it, but it slightly muddies the mechanism just before Benny's insight.
6. **The step changes material:** it creaks in Ch9 ("The step creaked"), so it is wooden, but in Ch10 it is "the wide stone step of the Great Barn".
7. **Brody's neckerchief goes home with Charlie** ("shawl and neckerchief and all"). It is his canon identifying visual. A child listener will ask, and the next story's illustrator needs it back. One line fixes it.
8. **Optional:** "You could cheer instead! I like it when people cheer." (Ch2) is still a shade self-centred for the founder's "never boastful" note. The surrounding "He meant it kindly" largely redeems it.

### Required revisions (Lock One, round 2)
1. **Fix the door direction in Ch8:** Buck (or Brody) takes the handle and draws the doors slowly open, consistent with Ch4.
2. **Settle the Ch10 close.** Either move the gathering to Buck's porch (with the lanterns arriving there and the rocking chair's first creak), or obtain explicit founder approval for a barn-step close in this story. In either case, replace the "Charlie handed to her mother by candle-lantern" image so it does not repeat S01-003, for example by having the lanterns bob away with Charlie asleep in Maggie Mae's arms, or with Brody carrying her home.
3. **Replace the surviving stock phrases** (Ch4 "long silence", Ch6 "eyes shone", Ch8 "big and warm… chest" and "biggest grin", Ch9 "rounder and rounder" and "better than he ever had before"). Drop "close but not too close" and "bow over one ear".
4. **Continuity fixes:** make the step one material (wood, given the creak); reword "haven't hung it out for years"; return Brody's neckerchief (or wrap Charlie only in the mother's shawl or Maggie Mae's quilt).

Keep total length at or above 5,000 narrated words after cuts.

### Fact checks (Sherlock)
- **Latch-string mechanism: VERIFIED** (general historical knowledge; my web search found no usable citation). The revision now describes it accurately: a leather string tied to an inside wooden latch, passed out through a hole above, so that pulling from outside lifts the latch out of its catch. A pivoting latch dropping into a notch or keeper is the authentic form. The "swollen with the damp" sticking is plausible for old wood in an autumn barn.
- **"The latch-string is (always) out": VERIFIED** as genuine American frontier hospitality idiom, current in the Appalachian region. Buck's gloss ("you needn't knock. Just pull the string and come in") is accurate.
- **Frost and harvest: NOW ACCURATE.** Apples tolerate a light frost; damage occurs around −2 °C (28 °F) and below with exposure. Winter squash and pumpkins for storage should be in before frost, and a hard frost damages them. Exposed dug potatoes are vulnerable. Sources: [UNL Extension](https://lancaster.unl.edu/frost-cold-temperatures-bring-end-gardening-season), [Fruit Growers News](https://fruitgrowersnews.com/news/tips-offered-to-handle-apple-frost-damage-during-harvest/), [SDSU Extension](https://extension.sdstate.edu/fall-frost-tolerance-common-vegetables).
- **Chipmunk climbing: ACCURATE.** Eastern chipmunks are ground-dwellers but capable climbers; "very good at staircases" is fair licence. Burrow entrances are about 1–2 in (2.5–5 cm), consistent with a chipmunk-sized hole. Sources: [WV DNR](https://wvdnr.gov/THE-EASTERN-CHIPMUNK), [Wikipedia](https://www.Wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamias_striatus).
- **Eagle vision in dim light: minor licence.** Eagles have exceptional daylight acuity, but their low-light vision is not notably better than other diurnal birds'. "An eagle's eyes would see best in the dim light" is a small stretch. It is acceptable in-world, but "see furthest" or "see sharpest" would be accurate.
