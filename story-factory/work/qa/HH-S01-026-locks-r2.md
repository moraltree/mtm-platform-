## LOCK ONE: "Piers Morgan" (story quality)

**Score: 8.7 / 10. PASS (pass mark 8.5)** (round 1: 8.1)

### What improved
- **Maverick now drives the climax.** "Then he caught sight of Maverick on the rail, and remembered" (l.419). Brody closes his eyes and plays "as if the porch were empty" (l.421). This turns round 1's most interesting unused idea into the story's emotional turning point. It also resolves Brody's urge to rush through the performance without anybody telling him to.
- **Rule 2 is now clean.** The route to the Pool is the children's (Brody reasoning from Charlie's and Maverick's clues, l.231), and so is the method (Benny's threes, l.272–286). Buck agrees, keeps the pace, tunes the banjo and remembers afterwards. He asks one question ("Which tune?", l.19).
- **Rule 1 is now clean.** The wordless tuning (l.217–221) is stronger than the maxim was, because the reader feels the patience instead of being told it. #40 lands at l.425 as the only telling in the story.
- **The reveal is earned.** The Ch1 hint (l.9), the fifth note "simply there, waiting" (l.301) and the paw going on "all by itself" (l.376) together make "You." (l.403) a fair surprise. With the sentimental framing cut, "It was mine," / "It was yours… now you've gone and fetched it back" carries the scene.
- **The close is fresh in its staging and in its payoff.**
  - The scene stays with Brody on the step while his folks talk with Buck on the porch bench (canon furniture from 010 and 013). No other S01 close has used this.
  - "It needs a name… So it doesn't get lost again" (l.442) pays off l.25's "a tune with no name is a hard thing to call back", so the naming is structurally motivated, not decorative. It is spoken and private, not an inscription (014, 015, 016, 017).
  - Brody names the tune after Buck (l.450), which reverses the reveal: the tune came from Brody, and now he gives Buck its name. Buck's answer is just to stop rocking for a moment (l.452), which rhymes with Ch8's "Buck had stopped rocking" (l.386).
  - The last image is the katydids keeping Brody's time and then carrying on "without him" (l.458). That is a gentle new twist on the nature-chorus close, and it ends on the Hollow rather than on sleep or a count.
  - The checks the coordinator asked for all clear: no banjo from its corner, no "porch went quiet", no Buck-alone ending, no falling-asleep close, no counting button.
- **The distinctive comedy and character work are intact:** the kingfisher, "three-and-a-halves", Beau "from the ground", "The banjo did not argue", Sadie Belle's "Play it as if it's going to stay", Rusty's slowed whistle and Reba's pie-humming.

### Remaining weaknesses
1. **A small logic slip in the final beat (l.456).** Brody plays "so softly that only Buck and the katydids could hear". But his folks are standing beside him with the lantern (l.454), so they would hear it too. One phrase fixes it. This is required but minor: see R2-1.
2. **The "eyes closed / as if the porch were empty" device appears three times in about 40 lines** (l.421, l.423, l.456). The Ch10 repeat is a deliberate echo, but three is one too many. Advisory: in Ch10, keep "as if the porch were empty" and drop "with his eyes closed", or the reverse.
3. **Brody's plan at the Pool is never quite closed off** (advisory). Brody's stated hope is that Buck will play or hum the tune if he fishes without thinking about it (l.231). Buck never does, and Benny's discovery overtakes the plan. This works as serendipity, but one half-line around l.264 ("Buck did not hum a note") would make the plan's quiet failure deliberate rather than forgotten.
4. **"Buck's Speed" as the name is very close to the theme** (advisory, not a Rule 1 fail). It comes after the Wisdom and names the *method*, not the moral. Brody's reasons at l.448 are memories (the four notes, Rusty's run, the beetles), not a lesson. I judge it a warm, earned callback to the Ch6 title. If the founder finds it too pointed, a softer name from the same memories would serve.
5. **Mild echoes in the season** (no action needed). Brody gives Buck something (the name) in a Brody-and-Buck story, as with 019's red trumpets. A short walk-home-with-family exit, as in 007 and 013. A nature chorus going on into the night (016, 017, 022). Each is handled differently enough, and none is the final staging beat in the same form.
