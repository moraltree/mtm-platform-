## Lock One

### "Piers Morgan", senior editor and literary critic

### Score: 7.6 / 10 — NOT YET PASSED (pass mark 8.5)

**What works.** The honesty arc has a real spine. The lie grows by itself and lands on the one creature Reba least wants to inconvenience. The confession is earned rather than extracted. Barney's "it's yours to tell" is the best idea in the draft. Buck does not solve anything, which is a genuine improvement on S01-005 and S01-006.

**Why it is not yet 8.5.** The brief asked for energetic comedy that escalates into a "hilarious muddle", and for inventive problem-solving. The comedy peaks early (Ch2 and Ch4) and is then swallowed by three chapters of guilt. The muddle does not escalate. It is presented once, as a list of returned baskets. The fix is the existing old-way float test made into a game, plus Reba's paws. That is pleasant, but it is not invention. Half the ensemble does nothing. The moral is stated four times. The porch close repeats lines and staging from S01-001, -005 and -006.

### Strengths
- **Reba's shortcuts are mostly her own.** "If you wanted to know whether a nut was sweet or bitter, the quickest way… was to crack one and eat it" (Ch2) is a food-driven shortcut that only Reba would take. "Last time I was eating" (Ch8) pays it off neatly. Rusty's cameo, "Taking a shortcut, are you?" (l.~96), is a deft way to acknowledge that the shortcut habit is canonically his while marking the difference. His bramble reflection is in character and is not preachy.
- **Comic set pieces that read aloud well:**
  - Reba's face "folded up like a paper bag… Her tongue tried to climb out of her mouth and escape" (Ch4).
  - "You usually touch everybody's supper" (l.243).
  - "Farewell, brave Captain Hollow-Nut. You floated well."
  - The Thunder of the Nut Shed.
  - "Can I have a hat?"
- **Barney's arc (Ch3, Ch6, Ch9).** The refused helper becomes the witness, then the companion, then Float Tub Officer. "No thank you, Barney" is planted, returned and apologised for. That is a satisfying shape.
- **The confession (Ch7) is very good.** It is specific, it comes in a rush and in the child's own words, and nothing in it is shaming. Buck's "That was a hard thing to say, wasn't it?" honours the effort, not the failure. "I'm glad not to be climbing that slope… These old knees" is warm and lightly funny.
- **The mother scene (Ch5, l.239–251).** It is tender and well judged: "If it turns out to be something more than tired, you know where I am."
- **Mountain Wisdom #97, "Small honesties keep the big troubles away."** It is quoted correctly from v1.0, has not been used earlier in S01, and fits the brief exactly.

### Weaknesses
1. **The comedy does not escalate (Ch2–4).** The brief's "increasingly hilarious muddle" becomes one cause (the shortcuts) and one reveal (the Ch4 returns list). Nothing compounds on stage. There is no chain reaction, no attempt by Reba to patch the muddle that makes it worse, and no comic topper. Ch4 is told largely in summary ("By the middle of the afternoon, half the Hollow had come back…").
2. **The tonal centre of gravity shifts to guilt (Ch5–7).** Three consecutive chapters dwell on Reba's misery: "felt hot all over", no supper, staring at the ceiling, "so heavy now that she could hardly breathe" (l.302). Each beat is good, but together they outweigh the comedy in a story briefed as "energetic comedy".
3. **The problem-solving is not inventive (Ch8).** The float tub was the miller's wife's existing method (Ch1). Barney's contribution is to make a game of it, which he offered in Ch1 (l.37). Reba's tactile sorting is a nice use of her gift, but it is presented as intuition ("I don't know how I know") rather than as a method she works out. Nobody solves the second half of the muddle: getting the right basket back to the right family when the first round of baskets has already been taken home and partly baked.
4. **The ensemble is thin.**
   - Sadie Belle, Beau and Maverick appear only as one line of porch furniture (l.484). Maverick, an eagle, "lay along the boards".
   - Benny carries a ladder and writes a hat.
   - Brody carries tools and asks two questions.
   - Rusty has one good cameo and then vanishes.
   - Charlie and Maggie Mae have small roles.

   Bible §10.5 and Canon 8 require distinct strengths to matter to the solution.
5. **The moral is told four times, plus the saying:**

### Fact checks (Sherlock)
- **Shagbark hickory (*Carya ovata*): correct in essentials.** The bark peels in long shaggy plates and the kernels are sweet and prized for baking. "Along the creek" is plausible, since shagbark grows in bottomlands as well as on slopes.
- **Bitternut hickory (*Carya cordiformis*): correct on taste.**
  - The kernel is intensely bitter (tannins) but **not poisonous**. The draft never claims it is, and "pull a face for a week" is acceptable comic hyperbole. Keep it that way: no "poison" wording in revision.
  - "Smooth grey trees" is acceptable. The bark is grey and tight, smoother than shagbark, becoming shallowly netted with age.
  - Its signature field mark is sulphur-yellow winter buds (a possible illustrator detail).
  - **Habitat note:** bitternut is most typically a moist bottomland and stream-bank tree, and shagbark is common on slopes. The draft's creek/slope split is not wrong (both occur in both settings), but it is the less typical way round. Optional: avoid stating a contrast, or swap.
- **"Look very nearly the same from the outside" (l.11) / "nearly the same size and nearly the same colour" (l.386): overstated.**
  - In the husk they differ clearly. Bitternut has a thin, yellowish, scurfy husk with four winged seams on its upper half. Shagbark has a thick, woody husk that splits to the base.
  - Out of the husk, a bitternut is usually smaller, rounder, often short-pointed, with a **thin** shell that cracks easily. A shagbark is larger, pale tan to whitish, and four-ridged and angular, with a **thick, very hard** shell.

  Reba's tactile cues are partly right: shagbark is heavier with a thicker shell, and bitternut is smoother, thinner and lighter. But **"rougher… thick, knobbly shells" is inaccurate for shagbark**, whose shell is fairly smooth and four-ridged. Suggested fix: "the shagbarks were bigger and heavier, with thick, hard shells ridged at the corners; the bitternuts were smaller and rounder, with thin shells that felt as if they would crack if you looked at them." Soften "nearly the same size" to "close enough to fool a hurried raccoon".
- **Weevils and empties: substantially correct.** Hickory nut weevil (*Curculio caryae*) larvae feed on the kernel inside the nut and leave frass ("dust"). **Caveat:** larvae usually chew out through a small round exit hole, so "look perfectly fine" (l.11) should be "look perfectly fine, apart from a pinprick of a hole you'd hardly notice". This also gives sharp-eyed Maverick a job (R4).
- **Float test: correct as folk practice.** Sound, full nuts sink, and empty or weevil-damaged nuts float. It is a standard sorting method for hickory nuts, pecans and acorns. Caveats worth one line each:
  1. It is not infallible: a few partly filled nuts float and the odd poor one sinks. Barney's "Last nut floating" game is fine.
  2. **Soaked nuts must be spread out to dry again before storage,** or they mould. The draft hands out the re-sorted nuts the same afternoon with no drying step. Add a line (it could be another ensemble job).
  3. The test is traditionally done soon after husking, before curing. Testing after drying, as here, is acceptable.
- **Cracking hickory nuts.** Shells, especially shagbark, are very hard and normally need a hammer-stone and anvil-stone. Reba, Brody and the miller's wife all crack nuts casually by paw. Add a nut-stone in the shed (a stone, not a blade, so there is no safeguarding issue).
- **Maggie Mae's cake by lunchtime (Ch4).** Cracking and picking enough hickory kernels for a cake, then baking and cooling it, all in one morning, is a stretch. Allow "by teatime", or have her bake with the first kernels she picks.
- **Raccoons and hickory nuts:** raccoons do eat them, correct. Rabbits and beavers do not much eat nuts, but this is a pooled community share, so no fix is needed.
- **Mountain Wisdom #97** is verbatim from v1.0 l.1650, and **correct**. (The draft inserts "young'uns" after it. If kept, it belongs before the saying or not at all.)

## Lock Three

### "Dr Noura" (child safeguarding, emotional safety, bedtime suitability) plus narration readiness

### Result: PASS WITH NOTES (conditional on S1–S5 and N1–N3)

### Frightening content
None of the frightening kind. Two worry beats need watching:
1. **The rumour of sick, "catching" trees and "no good nuts… ever".** Charlie's alarm (l.270) is resolved within one chapter, which is acceptable. Ensure C5 (retraction to the Hollow) lands so no listener is left with diseased trees.
2. **Reba's physical distress.** She "felt hot all over", "swallowed a bitternut whole and it had got stuck", and "so heavy now that she could hardly breathe" (l.302). "Could hardly breathe" is bodily-distress language that is too strong for a bedtime audience. Soften it, for example "so heavy she could hardly lift her chin".

### Imitation risks
- **Ladder (l.274).** Benny carries a ladder so that Buck can "look at the bark". This implies tree-climbing. Under the founder default (adults handle heights), and given Buck's age, remove the ladder, or make clear that only an adult would use it. Nobody climbs in the event, but the image is set up.
- **Float tub with small children (Ch8).** Charlie and "the littlest rabbits" play at a large tub of cold water. It is low risk, but no adult is stated as present for the re-sort. Seat the miller's wife in her chair supervising (it also gives her a role).
- **Raw-nut tasting.** Reba eating many nuts and tasting bitter cake is harmless. Bitternut is not poisonous, so there is no imitation hazard. Keep it non-poisonous in any revision.
- **Nut-cracking.** If a nut-stone is added (Sherlock), keep it as an adult-shown, paws-clear moment or off-page. No hammers on fingers for comedy.

### Reba's lie and its handling for young listeners
- **Strengths.** The fib is shown as born of embarrassment ("she could not make the words come out", l.211), not malice. The narrator says plainly that she made it up, so there is no ambiguity for a child. The consequences are natural rather than punitive: the rumour grows, and Buck is about to walk a hard slope. The confession is voluntary and accompanied (Barney: "I'll come with you. If you want."). It is met with warmth rather than scolding: Buck's "That was a hard thing to say", Charlie's hug. The relief is immediate and physical. She makes amends through work and apology, and she ends trusted again (taster, best-sorted Nut Day). This is good honesty modelling and it does not shame.
- **Concerns:**
  - **S1 — "Thank you… For not telling" (l.437)** teaches small listeners to value a witness keeping a friend's secret. That sits uneasily with safeguarding messaging ("if something's wrong, tell a grown-up"), especially since Barney's silence let the whole Hollow worry and Buck nearly climb a slope. Barney's motive is also partly wounded pride ("knew how it felt to be told you were in the way", l.124). **Revise:**
    - Reba thanks him "for letting me tell it myself".
    - Barney's reason is giving her the chance, not hurt feelings.
    - There is a visible limit, for example Barney was about to speak up when Buck set off ("I was going to tell Grandpappy before he got to the top. But I hoped you'd get there first.").
  - **S2 — "She had been greedy" (l.209).** This is a judgemental label for the character the child identifies with. Use "hungry" or "she had been thinking about her tummy", which is also funnier.
  - **S3 — Untruth count (see R2).** Five untruths in a row make lying look habitual and normal. One lapse is the healthier model for this age group, and it preserves the canon note.
  - **S4 — The apology at every basket (Ch9).** Saying "I'm sorry about the first one" to every family is touching but tips towards public penance. Consider one sincere apology to the gathered Hollow (which also covers C5) and a lighter touch at the baskets.

### Unkindness, and whether it is resolved
- "No thank you, Barney" (Ch1) is resolved by Reba's apology and the Float Tub Officer appointment. Good.
- Brody's "You couldn't feel it last time" is excused by the narrator and Reba answers it with good humour. Acceptable, but it is the one line close to a put-down. Consider giving it to Reba herself, as self-deprecation.
- Nobody shouts and nobody blames her. The community's kindness (Ch4: "Everybody was very kind about it") is good modelling.

### Moral shown, not preached
It is shown well in the action. It is preached in four extra places (see W5 and R1). Buck's Ch10 gloss before #97 is the most sermon-like passage in the story.

### Wind-down
Ch10 slows properly, with shorter sentences, the slow banjo, the drowsy tableau and the hat slipping over one eye (lovely). The rumbling tummy, "she had eaten only her share", is a good quiet echo. But the close is built from recycled furniture (R5), and the final image contradicts itself (the sign, R6). The story ends with a cut to an empty shed. Consider ending on a character drifting to sleep instead, which also breaks the five-story coda pattern.

### Narration readiness
- **N1 — l.280, "so that she would not have to say I did it."** The embedded speech needs quotation marks or italics in the manuscript, or a narrator will stumble.
- **N2 — l.102, Rusty's line, "Just, the miller's wife's way…"** This is awkward aloud. Suggest "Only, the miller's wife's way takes all week. I expect there's a reason."
- **N3 — Visual and odd images.**
  - "Very pale" Maggie Mae (l.170): use an expression instead.
  - Maverick "lay along the boards" (l.484): he should perch on the rail.
  - The capitalised signs (BRAVE FLOATERS, FLOAT TUB OFFICER) are stated in the text, so they work in audio. Fine.
- **Dialogue attribution** is clear throughout, including in the Barney/Reba bridge scene and the Ch7 group scene. "Mmmfff" (Ch4) reads aloud well.
- **Sentence length** suits read-aloud. The Ch7 confession is a long single speech by design. A narrator can carry it, and the urgency is intended.

### Required revisions (Lock Three)
1. **S1:** Reframe "for not telling" (l.437) and Barney's silence, with a stated limit and a non-sulking motive.
2. **S2:** Replace "greedy" (l.209).
3. **S3:** Reduce Reba to one untruth (shared with R2).
4. **S4:** Remove the ladder (l.274) or make it adult-only. Seat an adult at the float-tub scene.
5. **S5:** Soften "could hardly breathe" (l.302). Retract the tree rumour to the Hollow (C5).
6. **N1–N3** as above.
