## Lock One

### "Piers Morgan", senior editor and literary critic

### Score: 7.8 / 10 — NOT YET PASSED (pass mark 8.5)

This is the most original premise of the season so far. A line of set stones turns out to be a forgotten sunrise marker, and the children have to wait for the sun to "come home". That is a real idea, not a stock one. It gives patience something physical to do: the sun creeps a little way along the ridge each morning. The heron-and-dewdrop moment in Ch6 is the best single scene in the batch.

The draft is held below 8.5 by four things:
- a structural error in Ch7, where a paragraph has been pasted in the wrong place;
- adults supplying most of the key deductions, against the house lesson;
- the moral being stated four or five times, most heavily in Ch10;
- a closing chapter put together almost entirely from furniture already used in S01-001, -002, -004 and -005.

### Strengths
- **The premise and its mechanism.** The stones point at a notch, the sun misses it, and the sun moves a little each day. This is clear enough for a four-year-old and true enough to satisfy an adult (see Sherlock below). The first sunrise (Ch5) is a good false climax, and "Come back tomorrow" is a neat, honest hinge.
- **Ch6, "We'd never have seen any of this if we'd stayed in bed" (l.278–286).** The moral is shown through the senses: mist in golden curls, dew-lit webs "as if somebody had scattered it with tiny lamps", the heron "gold all down one side". Beau's change of heart is earned in one word ("Oh"). His wordless paw on her hoof "to make sure she had seen" is lovely, and it keeps the crush innocent and one-sided.
- **Beau is written to brief.** He is athletic (the somersault out of the holly, the stone-hopping), warm-hearted and slightly goofy without being stupid: "Mostly a racetrack," said Beau, from the grass. "I'm brilliant at very short ones" / "Then I'll be the patient one, and you can be the brilliant one" is a good character pairing. He also really does notice the notch, so he is a partner, not a sidekick.
- **Sadie Belle's sensitivity** comes through in action, not adjectives: "Other friends rushed. Sadie Belle noticed." There is her bark tally. And there is her solo worry in Ch7 ("What if they had all got up in the dark for nothing, because of her?"), resolved by thinking about the builders' certainty. That is a good, quiet interior beat.
- **Good comic touches:** Reba's "very old, very hard bread rolls"; Benny's "that was not a time, it was a feeling"; Maverick's "Eagles don't really do before dawn. We do after breakfast" and "nearly as good as me landing" (a good callback to S01-004); and Barney thanking Benny "very fast" and then pretending he had said something else.
- **Strong images:** the notch "like the gap where a tooth had come out"; the sun "like somebody walking very slowly along a fence"; the stones at the close "like a row of small moons".
- **Mountain Wisdom #40** is quoted correctly and fits the theme.

### Weaknesses
1. **Ch7 contains a misplaced paragraph (l.355–367).** After "She stood up and went home to bed early." (l.353), the text jumps to "He even bounded up the steps of Great-Grandpappy Buck's porch…". The "He" has no antecedent, because the last subject was Sadie Belle, and the scene is out of time order: it belongs in Beau's afternoon round, before Sadie Belle's evening worry. As it stands, Ch7 cannot be narrated. See R1.
2. **Adults supply the key deductions, against the house lesson that Buck must not solve the children's problem.**
   - Ch4 (l.152–172): Buck remembers "Something about the sun", then leads them to the answer with "What does that line point at?" and "And what comes up over Sunrise Ridge?" That is the central inference of the mystery, and Buck delivers it. The children only say it back in chorus.
   - Ch5 (l.233): Sadie Belle's knowledge that the sunrise moves is attributed to "Mama told me".
   - Ch5 (l.241–247): her mother provides the method ("Come back tomorrow").
   - Ch6 (l.290–292): her mother makes the prediction ("One more morning. Maybe two.") by reading Sadie Belle's tally.
   - The children contribute noticing and staying power. The adults contribute three of the four reasoning steps.
3. **The moral is stated far more than once.**
   - Sadie Belle: "Some secrets take a long time." (Ch2)
   - Buck: "You've only got to wait till they tell you." (Ch4)
   - Buck, mid-story aphorism: "That's how most good things get lost… a little bit at a time, when nobody's looking." (Ch8, l.402). Canon 2 says Buck's wisdom comes "only… at a story's close".
   - Beau: "She did the patient bit…" (Ch9)
   - Ch10 alone: "Waiting for somebody to come along who'd look properly. And then sit still. And then come back. And come back again… Not everybody would have kept coming back." Then "Funny how things come back when you go and stand in the right place." Then "Most of the wonders worth finding take a while, young'uns. Seeds. Sunrises. Friends." Only then the actual Mountain Wisdom.
   - By the time #40 arrives it lands as the fifth summary of the theme, not as a goodnight kiss.
4. **The ensemble is a round-robin, not a team (Ch3, l.97–125).** "But she did not know what they were for" and "But he did not know what they were for" is a formula. Benny's measuring and Rusty's "path that doesn't go somewhere" are nice characterisation, but neither moves the plot. Only Maverick's "It points at the ridges" is causal, and he dismisses it. In the long waiting section (Ch5–7) no friend contributes anything. Brody's whole role is a blanket, handing out cornbread, and one good question ("Who'll remember when it is?").
5. **The closing chapter recycles earlier stories.**
   - "It gave its first long, slow creak, and the porch went quiet" (l.450) is near-verbatim from S01-001 l.418 and S01-005 l.458.
   - "Bramble's quilt went over the smallest knees" is verbatim from S01-002 l.530.
   - "Buck took his banjo from its corner, and played a few soft notes" appears in S01-001, -002 and -005.
   - "Beau lay along the top step with his chin on his paws… Sadie Belle sat beside him, her long legs folded under her" (l.452) is a near-copy of S01-005 l.454.
   - The first star over Sunrise Ridge was used in S01-001, -002 and -004.
   - Buck reprising a hummed tune at the close is the emotional device of S01-005's ending.
   - "Fell asleep first, right in the middle of saying he was not tired" is a stock bedtime-book line.
   - The founder default is porch close "in most stories, staging varied". This is the third full-ensemble porch-at-dusk close of the same shape.
6. **The dawn-watch is itself a repeat from the previous story.** S01-004 already had a cold dawn vigil with mist lifting "in long white curls" and a wren beginning to sing. Here the mist lifts "in long, slow, golden curls" (l.278) and "a wren woke up and tried a few notes" (l.207). The vigil is essential to this story, so keep it, but its texture needs fresh observation.
7. **Repeated tics.**
   - The crush blush ("go a little pink under her headband, and was glad of the mist", l.76) is repeated at l.419 with "once again". It was also used in S01-003 and S01-005. Once is charming; twice plus "once again" reads as formula.
   - "Brilliant" is Beau's refrain (l.88, 90, 309, 321, 443). Five uses is about two too many.
   - "The creek talked (to itself)" appears three times.
   - "The sky turned from black to deep blue… the mist on the creek turned pink" (l.384) repeats Ch5 (l.207–211) almost word for word. A deliberate echo is fine, but here it reads as copy-paste.
8. **Even Day borrows the Harvest Home's signature ritual (Ch9).** v1.0 defines the Harvest Home as "a going-round of the whole Hollow in which each creature gives thanks, in turn". Ch9 is exactly that, and Buck frames Even Day as giving "thanks for the harvest coming in". That weakens both the new tradition and the canon festival. (See Lock Two.)
9. **Smaller craft points.**
   - Ch5 says the sun rose "a little way to the left of it… nowhere near the gap" (l.221). These two phrases contradict each other.
   - Who cleared the moss (l.478) is never shown, and it sits awkwardly against the Ch2 vow not to disturb the stones.
   - Buck says "young'uns" and then "little one" in consecutive sentences (l.474) when addressing a group.
   - Ch9's tune (the miller's wife's "old tune", which Buck joins) and Ch10's tune ("nobody on the porch had heard it before") are ambiguous. If they are the same tune, the text contradicts itself.

### Required revisions (Lock One)
1. **R1. Repair Ch7's structure.** Move the Buck's-porch paragraph group (l.355–367) into Beau's afternoon round, after Maverick and before "Back at the bend…", and give "He" a proper antecedent ("Last of all, Beau bounded up…"). Also fix the time order: Ch6 ends "That evening…" (l.290), and Ch7 then begins "that afternoon" (l.297). Make the mother's remark come first, or make Ch7 "the next afternoon" and adjust the count of mornings.
2. **R2. Let the children make the deductions.**
   - Ch4: cut Buck's "Something about the sun" and his two leading questions. Let Sadie Belle or Beau make the sun connection themselves, for example from the ridge's own name, or by building on Maverick's dismissed "It points at the ridges". Buck can remember only that his grandmother "used to take him down there early, once", ask one open question at most, and insist on a grown-up.
   - Ch6: Sadie Belle reads her own tally and the narrowing gap and makes the prediction herself. Her mother only confirms it.
   - Keep "Come back tomorrow" if you want, but it is stronger in Sadie Belle's mouth.
3. **R3. Give at least two friends a causal role in the investigation.** For example:
   - Benny proposes, and sets, a marker each morning (a peeled stick or a pebble on the ridge-line sighting), so the sun's daily creep can be measured. This turns "Beau was certain it was closer" into evidence and feeds R2's prediction.
   - Maverick flies up at dawn and reports how far the sun is from the notch.
   - Rusty knows the dawn path that avoids brambles (and gives Beau the right shortcut this time).
   - Cut the "But … did not know what they were for" formula in Ch3 to one use at most.
4. **R4. State the moral once.**
   - Cut the final two sentences of Buck's Ch8 speech (l.402), or give them to a child as an observation.
   - In Ch10, cut "Waiting for somebody… Not everybody would have kept coming back", "Funny how things come back…" and the "Most of the wonders worth finding… Seeds. Sunrises. Friends." prefix.
   - Let Buck deliver one Mountain Wisdom intact, with a consistent form of address. #40 is fine. #41 ("You can't rush a sunrise, so you might as well enjoy the dark before it.") fits the Ch6 heron scene better. Author's choice.
5. **R5. Rebuild the Ch10 close without recycled furniture.**
   - Replace the chair-creak sentence, the "smallest knees" quilt line, the "banjo from its corner" line, the Beau/Sadie top-step tableau, the first star over Sunrise Ridge, and the "fell asleep mid-sentence" ending.
   - Vary the staging. Options include a smaller porch group, or Buck walking the pair down to the cleared stones at dusk with a lantern carried by an adult. In either case still end with the rocking chair and the Wisdom.
   - If the remembered tune stays, make it clearly distinct from S01-005's tune-reprise and resolve the Ch9/Ch10 tune ambiguity.
6. **R6. Make Even Day its own tradition (founder confirmation needed; see Lock Two).**
   - Replace the Harvest Home-style thanks go-round with a distinct custom. Options: each creature names one thing they waited for; or the youngest sets one fresh white pebble at the line's foot; or everyone simply sits silent until the light reaches the rock.
   - Reword Buck's "to give thanks for the harvest coming in" (l.396) so the custom marks the turning of the year, not the harvest.
7. **R7. Freshen the dawn texture and pare the tics.**
   - Replace the wren and the "long curls" mist lines borrowed from S01-004.
   - Keep one crush blush only (cut "once again", l.419).
   - Reduce "brilliant" to two or three uses.
   - Vary the Ch8 sunrise description so it does not repeat Ch5 word for word.
   - Fix "a little way… nowhere near" (l.221), and show briefly who cleared the moss and why (or cut l.478's "with the moss cleared away").

### Fact checks (Sherlock)

| Claim in manuscript | Verdict |
|---|---|
| Sunrise Ridge is in the east and the sun comes up over it (l.166, 217) | **Correct**, per v1.0 ("Sunrise Ridge takes the first light in the east"). |
| The sun rises in a different place each day, far one way in summer and the other way in winter, moving "a little bit every morning" (l.233) | **Correct.** For a viewer facing east in the northern hemisphere, sunrise is north of east (left) in summer and south of east (right) in winter. Optional illustrator/narration note: "In summer it comes up way over there" should be her *left* hand and winter her *right*. |
| Before Even Day the sun rises left of the notch, then moves right each morning towards it (l.221, 254) | **Correct direction.** In the weeks before the September equinox, sunrise moves southward (right, facing east) every day. Well done. |
| The daily shift is visible morning to morning | **Correct, and the timing is plausible.** Near the equinox, sunrise azimuth changes fastest of the year: at southern-Appalachian latitudes (about 35–36°N) roughly 0.5° a day, about one sun-width. Six days from first sighting to the notch means the first miss was about 3° away (about six sun-widths). That fits "a little way to the left" but **not** "nowhere near" (l.221). Pick the former. Day 6 "just touching the edge" followed by "exactly in the middle" on day 7 is consistent with a notch about one sun-width wide. |
| Equinox sunrise "in the notch" and the notch due east | The text never says "due east", which is good. Strictly, equinox sunrise is due east only on a flat, level horizon. Over a raised ridge the sun first appears slightly south of due east (a degree or two at these latitudes). Because the builders aimed at the observed notch, the story is not affected. Recommend **not** adding "due east" in revision unless softened ("almost exactly east"). |
| "When the day and the night are the same length" (l.396) | **Acceptable simplification.** Day and night are *roughly* equal at the equinox; exactly equal daylight falls a few days after the September equinox at these latitudes, and a ridge-ringed valley shortens actual sunlit hours. Buck's folk definition is fine for a children's story. Do not make it more precise. |
| Light runs "down the line… one after another… until the light reached the flat rock" (l.390) | **Poetic licence, technically reversed.** At sunrise, higher ground is lit first; the rock at the top of the bank would catch the light a moment before the stones at the water's edge. The difference is seconds and the image is lovely. Optional fix: let the light reach the rock and then run *down* the stones to the creek, or have them all kindle "at once". |
| "So bright that they had to squint" | True. See the Lock Three sun-safety note. |
| First star "exactly above the notch" at dusk (l.480) | Plausible (the eastern sky darkens first), but recycled (Lock One W5). |
| Mallard-type family "one big and six small" at the equinox (l.278) | **Incorrect.** Ducklings hatch in spring and early summer and are fully grown by late September. Change to, for example, "a mother duck and her six nearly grown young", or simply "a line of ducks". |
| Heron standing on one leg in the shallows at dawn | Correct (great blue heron, present year-round in the southern Appalachians). |
| Wren singing at dawn in autumn | Correct (Carolina wren sings year-round), but borrowed from S01-004. |
| Dew-strung spider webs in early autumn | Correct (peak orb-weaver season). |
| Holly bush in the woods; dandelion in late September | Correct (American holly is native; dandelions re-flower in autumn). |
| First yellow hickory leaves at the equinox | Acceptable: the very first colour, not peak. |
| White stones not found in the creek, so carried in | Plausible (quartz cobbles from elsewhere). Fine. |
| "Eagles don't really do before dawn" | Fine as character comedy (eagles are diurnal). |

## Lock Three

### "Dr Noura" (child safeguarding, emotional safety, bedtime suitability) plus narration readiness

### Result: PASS WITH NOTES (conditional on S1–S4)

### Frightening content
- There is nothing frightening at the climax or the close. Tension is "worry, not fear" throughout (Beau's grumbling on the fifth morning; Sadie Belle's private worry, which she resolves herself). This complies with §15.
- Barney's "teeth of a buried giant" (l.115) briefly frightens Charlie, and it is resolved in the same sentence ("only joking… definitely stones"). It is mid-story and acceptable. Optional: soften to "a buried giant's marbles" so there is no fright at all.
- Buck's "That's how most good things get lost" carries a faint melancholy. Cutting it per R4 also helps the bedtime tone.

### Imitation risks
- **S1. Solo dawn creek-bank walks (Ch1, l.3).** Sadie Belle habitually walks Silver Creek "in the early autumn mornings, when the mist was still lying on the water and nobody else was about". The rest of the story is careful that sunrise at the creek needs a grown-up. Buck: "Sunrise is an early hour for a creek bank." Ch1 shows the opposite as a cherished habit. Fix: set her walk on the path near home "where Mama could see her from the window", or later in the morning once the Hollow is up. Keep her a little way back from the water.
- **S2. Supervision on mornings 2–6 must be explicit.** Ch5 shows the mother present and Ch7 implies it. Add one line in Ch6 ("Every morning, Mama came too, with the blanket and the flask"). Sadie Belle's solo late-afternoon sit at the creek (Ch7, l.345) is daytime, on the bank top, at the rock. It is acceptable, but could easily be set within sight of the mill.
- **S3. Beau alone in the dark (Ch5, l.197–201).** He oversleeps, runs alone before dawn and takes an unknown shortcut through brambles. It is comic, but it models a child out alone in the dark on an untried route. Fix: Beau's mother or father brings him, still late and still with a bramble on his tail; or Rusty's family walks him by the proper path.
- **S4. Looking at the sun.** The whole story is about sunrise-watching, so children will copy it. "So bright that they had to squint" (l.217) is the only safety cue. Add one natural line, for example Mama: "Watch the notch and the stones, not the sun itself. It's too bright for eyes." Or have the children watch the light run down the stones rather than stare into the sun. This costs nothing and protects small eyes.
- **Adults handle flames:** the porch lantern is lit (passive voice, l.450). Fine, but "Buck lit the lantern" would make it explicit at no cost.
- Stone-hopping on mossy stones (Ch2) happens on grass well up from the water, and the slip is harmless. Acceptable.
- Buck's whittling knife is used by an adult. Fine.

### Unkindness, and whether it is resolved
- Maverick's "I don't think it's anything important. If it were important, I'd know" is brash but not unkind, and he is redeemed ("thankful for mornings, actually").
- Beau speaks "quite crossly" on the fifth morning, and Sadie Belle answers with kindness, not reproach. This is well handled.
- Benny calling Barney's idea "ridiculous" is mild sibling friction, typical of the series. Acceptable.
- There is no humour at anyone's expense. Beau's tumbles are laughed at *with* him ("Beau, hearing her laugh, began laughing too").

### Moral shown, not preached
Ch6 shows the moral beautifully. The ending preaches it (see Lock One W3 and R4). From a bedtime perspective, a long, reflective Buck speech before the Wisdom slows the wind-down and turns the close into a lesson. Trim it.

### Wind-down
- Ch10 lowers the energy well: tired friends, a soft tune, dusk, the stones "like a row of small moons", eyes closing.
- The final image of the stones waiting for the sun "to come home again next year" is soothing and hopeful.
- Once the recycled lines are replaced (R5), keep the same slow cadence.

### Narration readiness
- **N1 (blocking).** Ch7's misplaced paragraph (R1). A narrator will stumble on "He even bounded…" straight after Sadie Belle goes to bed.
- **N2.** The double possessives are hard to read aloud: "Sadie Belle's mother's arm" (l.376) and "Sadie Belle's mother's flask" (l.409). Use "her mother's arm" with the subject restructured, "Mama's flask", or give the mother a name (founder item 4).
- **N3.** The Ch8 sunrise paragraph repeats Ch5's sentences closely (l.384 vs l.207–211). A narrator will hear it as a mistake rather than an echo. Vary it, or make the echo clearly deliberate (a refrain with one changed word).
- **N4.** Dialogue attribution is clear throughout, including the Ch9 round and the Ch9 "next year" exchange (each line tagged). The Ch3 list of guesses is reported speech and reads easily.
