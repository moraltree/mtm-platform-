## LOCK THREE — "Dr Noura" (child development and safeguarding) and narration readiness

**Verdict: CONDITIONAL PASS. Two small safety fixes and one softening.**

**Resolved from r1:**
- S1: a grown-up handles the water butt and Brody only carries a bucket ✓.
- S2: the woodchuck uses her own shears and Sadie Belle holds the basket ✓.
- S3: the line is "well back, and off to one side of the way the bees flew in and out" (l.258), and the speaking-line references are updated at l.273, 277, 283 and 287 ✓.
- S4: Charlie's mother walks ahead with the lantern (l.344) ✓.
- E1: the tears and "passed on" are gone, and the remaining grief is held gently ✓.

**Required:**
- **S5. l.314:** "Charlie sat on the stepping stone nearest to the hub, as close as the rule allowed." The stepping stone nearest the hub is right beside the gums, which contradicts the new line placed well back and off to one side. This is the one reference the update missed. Fix: "Charlie sat on the path, right at Buck's line, as close as the rule allowed, and listened to it."
- **S6. l.130:** "the whole line sat down on the path in a heap, one on top of another, with Charlie at the very bottom". This puts six bigger friends, including a bear cub, on top of the smallest chipmunk. The scene is gentle and she is clearly fine ("Did we win?"), but it is the one image in the story a small listener might worry about, and it models the smallest getting squashed. Fix: "with Charlie sitting on the very top, saying, 'Did we win?'" Falling backwards makes the back of the line land first, so putting her on top needs nothing more than the change of words. The button line becomes even funnier.
- **E2. l.206** (r1 item 12, partly done): "When she was gone, nobody told the bees. And they went." This is still a direct cause and effect told to children by a trusted adult: "if you forget to tell, they leave". Add one softening clause: "And whether it was that, or just that nobody was minding them, they went."

**Advisory:**
- In the root chain, "Charlie took hold of Reba's tail" (l.130) is affectionate slapstick. Consider "Reba's waist" so that tail-pulling is not modelled. This is a minor matter of taste.
- Brody carrying the tadpole bucket to the Beaver Ponds by himself in daylight is acceptable. Optionally, "with his father beside him".

### Narration readiness
- **READY once the fixes above are made.** The l.219 split (now l.223–225) helps. The root paragraph (l.130, about 190 words) reads well as one breathless build, and the run of "and… and…" suits a chain gag. Keep it as one paragraph, with a pause before "Did we win?"
- After the fix at l.204 the narrator is no longer left guessing who "her" refers to.
- Pronunciation notes are as in r1 ("billhook", "bee gums", "lemon balm").
